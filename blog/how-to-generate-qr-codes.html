How to Generate QR Codes for Free (2026 Guide)

QR codes are everywhere — restaurant menus, payment terminals, event tickets, product packaging, WiFi login cards. They bridge the physical and digital worlds: one scan with a phone camera, and the user lands exactly where you want them. The good news: generating QR codes is completely free and takes seconds. This guide covers everything — from basic URL codes to WiFi and contact-card codes — plus how to scan them.

What is a QR code?

QR (Quick Response) codes are two-dimensional barcodes invented in Japan in 1994. Unlike traditional barcodes that hold ~20 characters, a QR code can store over 4,000 alphanumeric characters. Every modern smartphone camera scans them natively — no special app needed. The three large squares in the corners help cameras orient and read the code even at angles or in poor light.

What can you put in a QR code?

  • Website URLs: The most common use — link to your site, portfolio, menu, or social profile. Keep URLs short for cleaner, easier-to-scan codes.
  • WiFi credentials: Cafes and offices print QR codes so guests join WiFi without typing long passwords. Format: WIFI:T:WPA;S:NetworkName;P:password;;
  • Contact cards (vCard): Encode your name, phone and email so scanners save you as a contact in one tap — perfect for business cards.
  • Plain text: Short messages, coupon codes, or instructions.
  • Email/SMS: Pre-addressed email or text message links.
  • App download links: Point directly to your Play Store or App Store listing.

QR code best practices

  • Size matters: Print QR codes at least 2 × 2 cm (about 1 inch). Billboards and banners need much larger codes relative to viewing distance.
  • Keep contrast high: Black on white scans most reliably. Fancy colored codes look nice but can fail in poor light.
  • Don't overload content: Shorter content = simpler pattern = faster, more reliable scanning. Use a URL shortener for long links.
  • Always test: Scan your printed code with 2–3 different phones before mass printing.
  • Add a call to action: A code alone confuses people — add "Scan to view menu" or "Scan for WiFi" nearby.
  • Quiet zone: Leave white space around the code (at least 4 modules wide) — scanners need it.

Static vs. dynamic QR codes

Our generator creates static QR codes — the content is baked into the pattern permanently. They're free forever and never expire. Dynamic codes (offered by paid services) let you change the destination URL later and track scans, but require a subscription and route scans through the provider's servers. For most uses — menus, business cards, WiFi, packaging — static codes are the right choice: free, private, permanent.

How to scan a QR code

  • iPhone: Open the Camera app and point it at the code — a notification appears. (iOS 11+)
  • Android: Most camera apps scan automatically; otherwise use Google Lens.
  • From an image file: Got a QR code as a screenshot or photo? Upload it to our QR Code Reader — it decodes the content instantly in your browser, no app install needed.

Creative uses you might not have tried

  • Event check-ins: Unique codes per ticket, scanned at the door.
  • Product manuals: Print a code on the box linking to video instructions.
  • Restaurant tables: Per-table codes linking to the menu or bill-pay page.
  • Real estate signs: Link to virtual tours and photo galleries.
  • Homework handouts: Teachers link to supplementary videos and quizzes.
  • Wedding invitations: Link to the venue map, gift registry or photo album.

QR codes in marketing: do they work?

When used well, yes. The key is removing friction: a QR code that opens a pre-filled order form converts far better than one linking to a generic homepage. Track performance by using unique URLs per placement (e.g., yoursite.com/menu?src=table12) so analytics show which codes get scanned. And remember the golden rule of QR marketing — always give people a reason to scan. "Scan for 10% off" outperforms a bare code every time. Frequently asked questions

Do QR codes expire?

Static QR codes (like the ones our generator makes) never expire — the pattern permanently encodes your content. Only paid "dynamic" codes can stop working if you cancel the subscription.

Can I use QR codes commercially?

Yes — QR codes you generate are yours to use on packaging, advertising, business cards and anywhere else, free of charge.

How much data can a QR code hold?

Up to ~4,296 alphanumeric characters, but shorter is better for reliable scanning.
