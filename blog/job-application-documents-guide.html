How to Prepare Your Documents for Online Job Applications (2026 Guide)

You found the perfect job, filled out the long application form, and then — the upload page rejects your CV. Wrong format. File too big. Photo the wrong dimensions. Documents like these cause an embarrassing number of applications to fail before a human ever reads them. The fixes are all simple, and every one of them can be done free in your browser. Here is the complete checklist for preparing job application documents that pass every upload form and look professional when they land in a recruiter's inbox.

1. Always submit PDF, never Word (usually)

Unless the posting explicitly asks for a Word document, submit your CV as a PDF. PDFs look identical on every device — your careful formatting survives — while Word files can reflow, lose fonts, or display differently depending on the recruiter's software version. Most applicant tracking systems (the software that first scans your CV) also parse PDFs reliably.

If your CV is in Word or Google Docs, export it: in Word, File → Save As → PDF; in Google Docs, File → Download → PDF. Keep one master copy in the editable format and export a fresh PDF each time you tailor the CV, so you never accidentally send an outdated version.

2. Beat the file-size limit before it beats you

Many application portals cap uploads at 1–5 MB, and a CV with a photo, logos, or scanned certificates can easily exceed that. If your file is too big, don't just delete content — compress it. Our PDF Compressor shrinks oversized PDFs to fit upload limits while keeping the text sharp and readable, entirely in your browser with no signup.

Prevention is better than cure: keep photos in your CV small (a 3×4 cm headshot doesn't need to be 5 megapixels), avoid embedding huge background graphics, and export with standard — not print — quality settings when the document is for screen reading.

3. Merge related documents into one PDF

Recruiters prefer one tidy file over five attachments named "Document1.pdf" through "Document5.pdf". Combine your CV, cover letter, and key certificates into a single PDF in the right order: cover letter first, then CV, then certificates. Our PDF Merger combines multiple PDFs in your browser — just arrange them in order and download the merged result.

Don't merge everything you own. One or two pages of CV, one page of cover letter, and only the certificates relevant to this specific job. A 40-page mega-file signals that you can't prioritize — a focused 4–6 page package signals the opposite.

4. Convert photos and scans to clean PDFs

Job portals often ask for scanned copies of degrees, CNIC/passport copies, or experience letters — and ask for them as PDFs, not phone photos. If you only have photos of these documents, convert them properly instead of uploading the raw image. Our Images to PDF tool turns your photos into a clean, properly-sized PDF document.

For the best results, photograph documents in bright, even light, hold the phone directly above the page (not at an angle), and make sure all four corners are visible before converting. A crooked, shadowy scan of your degree looks careless — and first impressions are exactly what these documents are.

5. Get the profile photo right

Where a photo is required or customary, treat it like a passport photo's professional cousin: plain background, good lighting, shoulders up, neutral expression, recent. The most common technical failure is size — portals that accept photos often want them under a few hundred kilobytes. Run yours through our Image Compressor to hit the limit without visible quality loss, and check the required dimensions before uploading (many forms specify exact pixel sizes).

Use the same photo across your CV, LinkedIn, and the application portal. Consistency looks professional and makes you recognizable; three different photos across three documents looks disorganized.

6. Name your files like a professional

Recruiters download dozens of CVs into one folder. "CV-final-FINAL-v3.pdf" is invisible in that folder; "Ahmed-Khan-CV-2026.pdf" is findable. Use this format:

  • YourName-CV-2026.pdf — your CV
  • YourName-Cover-Letter-CompanyName.pdf — tailored cover letter
  • YourName-Certificates.pdf — merged certificates

No spaces (use hyphens), no special characters, no version numbers. And double-check that the file you attached is actually the file you meant — attaching last year's CV to this year's dream job is a classic, painful mistake.

7. Keep your CV machine-readable

Before a human sees your CV, software usually does. Applicant tracking systems parse your PDF to extract skills, dates, and job titles — and they fail on fancy designs. Keep these rules:

  • Use standard section headings: "Work Experience", "Education", "Skills" — creative headings like "My Journey" confuse parsers.
  • Avoid text inside images, text boxes, and complex tables — parsers often can't read them.
  • Use a common font (Arial, Calibri, Times New Roman) and avoid headers/footers for critical info like your phone number.
  • Save as a text-based PDF, not a scanned image of a printed CV — scanned images are unreadable to most parsers.

A quick test: open your PDF, try to select a sentence with your mouse, and copy it. If you can copy clean text, the parser can read it too.

8. Mind the cover letter length

One page, three to four short paragraphs: why this role, why you, and a confident close. Hiring managers skim, and a two-page letter rarely gets fully read. Draft it, then check the length with our Word Counter — aim for roughly 200–350 words. If you're over, cut adjectives before you cut content; if you're under 150 words, you probably haven't said enough about why you fit this specific job.

9. The pre-submit checklist

Run through this before every submission — it takes two minutes and catches the errors that cost interviews:

  • Correct file attached (open it and glance at the first page)
  • File is PDF, under the size limit, with a professional filename
  • Your name, phone number, and email are current and typo-free
  • Dates and job titles match across CV, cover letter, and LinkedIn
  • Certificates merged in order, each one legible
  • Photo meets the size and format requirements
  • Spell-check the whole package once more — tools help, but read it yourself too

Your documents are your first interview

None of this is about gaming the system — it's about removing the silly, technical reasons good candidates get filtered out before anyone reads their story. A clean PDF under the size limit, with your name in the filename and your certificates in order, tells the recruiter one thing before they've read a word: this person is careful. In a pile of hundreds of applications, that first impression is worth real effort.
