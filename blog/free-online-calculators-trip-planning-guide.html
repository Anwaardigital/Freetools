Plan a Trip Like a Pro: 6 Free Online Calculators That Do the Math for You (2026)

Planning a trip means answering a stream of small numerical questions, and getting any of them wrong costs real money. How much is that hotel in your own currency? Is the 25%-off deal actually cheaper than the fixed-price option? How much should you tip at that restaurant abroad? How many days are you actually spending in each city? What time does your flight land in local time? How many kilometers is that "2 miles to the beach"?

None of these questions is hard, but there are dozens of them, and mental math on the go is where budgeting mistakes are born. The six free calculators below run entirely in your browser — no signup, no app install, nothing sent to a server — and they answer every one of these questions in seconds. Work through them in order while planning, and your trip budget stops being a guess.

Calculator 1: The currency converter (know what everything really costs)

Every price abroad exists in two numbers: the number on the tag and the number in your bank account. A dinner listed at a price that "sounds cheap" can be shockingly expensive once converted, and the reverse happens too — travelers routinely overpay for things that would be bargains at home, simply because the conversion felt like too much work in the moment.

Before the trip, run your big planned expenses — hotels, intercity transport, tour prices — through the Currency Converter and write down the home-currency equivalents next to each item in your itinerary. This does two things: it builds a realistic total budget, and it calibrates your instincts. After converting twenty or thirty prices, you start to "feel" the exchange rate, and on-the-spot decisions get faster and more accurate. During the trip, keep the converter bookmarked on your phone for restaurant menus and shop prices — a five-second check beats a nasty surprise on your card statement.

Pro tip: exchange rates move daily, so convert the big items a day or two before you book, not a month before. For the small daily stuff, a rough rounded rate in your head is fine.

Calculator 2: The tip calculator (tip correctly everywhere, without awkward math)

Tipping customs change by country — in some places 10% is generous, in others 20% is expected — and the bill always arrives when you're relaxed, chatting, and least inclined to do arithmetic. Undertipping feels bad; overtipping burns your budget silently over a two-week trip.

Look up the local norm once (your hotel staff will tell you honestly if you ask), then use the Tip Calculator to get the exact total. The real value of a tip calculator isn't the math — it's the splitting. When the bill is shared among four or six people with different orders, the calculator keeps it fair in seconds and avoids the classic end-of-dinner scene where everyone stares at the receipt. Screenshot the result and settle up immediately; "we'll sort it out later" is how friendships get taxed.

Calculator 3: The discount calculator (spot real deals, skip fake ones)

Tourist areas are full of sales: "25% off," "buy 2 get 1 free," "extra 10% off the sale price." Some are genuine bargains; others are priced so the "discount" just returns the item to its normal price. The difference between the two is one subtraction and one percentage — exactly the calculation your brain skips when a shopkeeper is watching.

Before you buy, run the numbers through the Discount Calculator and compare the result against the price back home (which you already converted with Calculator 1). Stacked discounts are the trickiest — a 20% discount followed by an extra 10% off is not 30% off, it's 28% off, because the second discount applies to the already-reduced price. The calculator does this correctly every time. A useful rule: if the "deal" price after discount is within a few percent of the home price, it's not a deal — it's a souvenir with a story.

Calculator 4: The date-duration calculator (plan days, not guesses)

Trip planning runs on days, and day-counting is where itineraries quietly break. Is that hotel booking 7 nights or 8? If you arrive on the 12th and leave on the 19th, how many full days do you actually have in the city? People routinely mis-count by one — and one day is 15% of a week-long trip.

Use the Date Duration calculator for every leg of the trip: arrival to departure in each city, total trip length, and days between bookings. The pattern that works best: count nights for hotels and full days for activities, separately. A 7-night stay with a morning arrival and an evening departure gives you 7 full days — but the same 7 nights with an evening arrival and a morning departure gives you only 6. The calculator removes the ambiguity; your itinerary stops drifting by a day.

Pro tip: also use it backwards — if a tour says "5-day trek starting Monday," verify which day it actually ends before booking the flight home.

Calculator 5: The time zone converter (land at the right local time)

Nothing ruins the first day of a trip like time-zone confusion. You land "at 3 PM" — but whose 3 PM? Your connecting flight boards at a time shown in the departure city's zone, your hotel check-in is in the arrival city's zone, and your phone may take an hour to catch up after landing.

Before flying, convert every critical time with the Timezone Converter: departure time, connection boarding, arrival time, hotel check-in, and any pre-booked tour pickup. Write each one down with the city name attached ("pickup: 09:00 Cairo time"), not just a number. The most expensive mistake here is the return flight — travelers have missed flights home by converting the wrong direction. Double-check the return leg separately; it's the one that costs the most to fix.

Calculator 6: The unit converter (miles, gallons, pounds — decoded)

Travel is a crash course in other countries' measurement systems. Road signs in miles, fuel in gallons, luggage limits in pounds, temperatures in Fahrenheit, room sizes in square feet — and your brain, trained on kilometers and kilograms, has to interpret them all while jet-lagged.

The Unit Converter handles all of it: distance for driving days, weight for airline baggage limits (overweight fees are brutal, so convert before the airport), volume for fuel planning on road trips, and temperature so you pack the right clothes. The highest-value conversion is baggage: weigh your bag at home, convert to the airline's units, and leave a margin — airport scales are optimistic about everyone except you.

Putting it together: the 15-minute pre-trip routine

The six calculators are most powerful as a routine, not as one-offs. About a week before departure, sit down for fifteen minutes and run the sequence:

1. Convert all major costs to home currency with the Currency Converter and finalize the budget. 2. Verify every leg's day count with Date Duration and lock the itinerary. 3. Convert all flight and pickup times with the Timezone Converter and save them with city names. 4. Check baggage weight against the airline limit using the Unit Converter. Then, during the trip, keep the Tip Calculator and Discount Calculator bookmarked on your phone for daily decisions.

Fifteen minutes of calculation before the trip replaces a week of mental math during it — and the savings from catching one bad deal or one mis-counted day usually pay for the whole routine many times over.

Frequently asked questions

Do these calculators need an internet connection?

They run in your browser, so load the pages once while you have a connection; most will keep working from the cached page. For currency conversion, rates are refreshed when you're online — convert the important figures before you leave coverage.

Is my data sent anywhere when I use these tools?

No. Every calculator on this site runs entirely in your browser — the amounts you type never leave your device. That's the point of the privacy-first design: a budgeting tool shouldn't need your financial details on someone else's server.

Why not just use a phone app for this?

You can, but apps want installs, permissions, accounts, and often subscriptions for the same math a web page does instantly. These calculators open in one tap, work on any device, and ask for nothing in return.

Which calculator saves travelers the most money?

In our experience, the currency converter and the date-duration calculator prevent the costliest mistakes — overpaying through bad conversions and mis-planning days. The tip and discount calculators save smaller amounts daily, which adds up over a long trip.
