How to Create a Favicon Online for Free (2026 Guide)

That tiny icon in your browser tab — the one next to the page title — is called a favicon, and it's one of the smallest branding decisions on your website. Sites without one show a generic blank-page icon, which quietly signals "unfinished" to visitors. The good news: making a favicon takes about a minute. This guide shows you how to turn any image into a set of properly sized favicons with our free Favicon Generator, and how to add them to your website.

What is a favicon, and why does it matter?

A favicon (short for "favorites icon") is the small image browsers display in tabs, bookmarks, history lists, and sometimes search results. It's typically shown at just 16×16 pixels — tiny — but it's visible on every single page of your site. A custom favicon makes your tabs recognizable at a glance, helps users find your site among twenty open tabs, and completes the professional look of a finished website.

You don't need to be a designer. A simple bold shape, a single letter on a colored background, or a simplified version of your logo works perfectly at these sizes.

What the free favicon generator does

The Favicon Generator takes any image you upload and produces favicon PNGs in four standard sizes:

  • 16×16 — the classic browser tab and bookmark icon size.
  • 32×32 — used by modern browsers for tabs and taskbar shortcuts on high-density displays.
  • 48×48 — used for desktop shortcuts on some systems.
  • 180×180 — the Apple touch icon size, shown when someone saves your site to their iPhone or iPad home screen.

Everything happens in your browser — your source image is never uploaded to a server.

Step-by-step: generate your favicons

  1. Choose a source image. Pick something simple and bold: a logo mark, an initial letter, or a clean icon. Square images work best. Avoid detailed photos — detail disappears at 16 pixels.
  2. Open the Favicon Generator. It runs in any modern browser; nothing to install.
  3. Upload your image. Select the file from your device. The tool stays entirely local — nothing is transmitted.
  4. Generate the icon set. The tool produces the 16, 32, 48, and 180px PNG versions of your image.
  5. Download the files. Save each size. Keep the filenames simple and consistent, for example favicon-16.png, favicon-32.png, favicon-48.png, and apple-touch-icon.png.
  6. Upload them to your website. Place the files in your site's root directory or an images folder, alongside your other site files.
  7. Add the HTML (see below). Reference the icons in the <head> of your pages so browsers know to use them.

Adding favicons to your website: the HTML

Once your icon files are on your server, add these lines inside the <head> section of your HTML pages (adjust the paths to match where you put the files):

<link rel="icon" type="image/png" sizes="16x16" href="/favicon-16.png">
<link rel="icon" type="image/png" sizes="32x32" href="/favicon-32.png">
<link rel="icon" type="image/png" sizes="48x48" href="/favicon-48.png">
<link rel="apple-touch-icon" href="/apple-touch-icon.png">

Browsers pick the size they need automatically. The apple-touch-icon line covers iPhones and iPads when visitors add your site to their home screen. If your site uses a shared header template, add these lines once and every page gets the favicon.

Not seeing it? Browsers cache favicons aggressively. After adding yours, do a hard refresh (Ctrl+Shift+R on Windows, Cmd+Shift+R on Mac) or open the page in a private/incognito window to check.

Design tips: what makes a good favicon

  • Keep it extremely simple. At 16×16 pixels there is no room for detail. One letter, one simple shape, or a two-color mark beats a full logo every time.
  • Use high contrast. A white letter on a solid colored square reads clearly; thin gray lines on white disappear.
  • Test at actual size. Shrink your source image to 16 pixels wide in your head — if you can't tell what it is, simplify it before generating.
  • Match your brand color. Using your site's primary color as the favicon background ties the tab icon to your brand instantly.
  • Avoid transparency edges. If your source has a transparent background, make sure the visible shape is bold enough to survive the downscale — faint edges turn into gray mush at 16px.
  • One concept across sizes. Use the same source image for all sizes so your tab icon, bookmark icon, and home-screen icon are recognizably the same mark.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Using a full detailed logo. A logo with a tagline becomes an unreadable smudge at favicon size. Extract just the icon or initial.
  • Forgetting the Apple touch icon. The 180px version is what iPhone users see when they save your site to their home screen — don't skip it.
  • Wrong file paths in the HTML. If the favicon doesn't appear, the most common cause is a path that doesn't match where the files actually are. Double-check the href values.
  • Only testing in one browser. Check your tab icon in at least Chrome and one other browser, plus a phone home-screen save if you can.

Frequently asked questions

Is the favicon generator free?

Yes — no signup, no limits, and the icons you download are yours to use on any website.

What image should I start from?

A square PNG of your logo mark or a simple icon, ideally at least 512×512 pixels. Larger, cleaner source images downscale better than small ones.

Do I still need a .ico file?

Modern browsers all accept PNG favicons, which is what this tool generates. The classic favicon.ico format still works but is no longer required for current browsers.

Why isn't my new favicon showing?

Almost always caching. Hard-refresh the page, clear your browser cache for the site, or test in an incognito window. Also verify the file paths in your HTML <link> tags.
