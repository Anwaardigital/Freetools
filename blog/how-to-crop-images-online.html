How to Crop Images Online for Free (2026 Guide)

The photo is great — except for the stranger in the corner, the messy background, or the extra whitespace around a screenshot. Cropping cuts an image down to exactly the region you want, removing distractions and focusing attention. This guide shows you how to crop images online for free with pixel-precise control, right in your browser.

When cropping is the right move

  • Remove distractions: cut out photobombers, clutter, or irrelevant edges.
  • Focus the subject: tighten a wide shot so the important part fills the frame.
  • Clean up screenshots: trim browser chrome, taskbars, and empty space around the part that matters.
  • Fix composition: reframe a shot after the fact — center the subject, apply the rule of thirds.
  • Extract a detail: pull one chart, face, or product out of a larger image.

What you'll need

Any modern browser, desktop or mobile. No installation, no account. Cropping runs entirely on your device — no internet needed after the page loads.

Step-by-step: crop an image online

  1. Open the Image Cropper. You'll see an image selection area.
  2. Select your image. A full preview appears so you can see exactly what you're working with.
  3. Enter the crop region using the four number fields:
    • X — how many pixels from the left edge the crop starts.
    • Y — how many pixels from the top edge the crop starts.
    • Width — how wide the cropped region is, in pixels.
    • Height — how tall the cropped region is, in pixels.
    The fields default to a 400×400 region (or smaller, if your image is smaller); the maximums are capped at your image's actual dimensions so you can't crop outside the picture.
  4. Click "Crop & Download". The cropped region downloads immediately as a PNG, named after your original — e.g. photo-cropped.png.

Understanding X, Y, Width, Height

Think of it as drawing a rectangle on your image. X and Y place the rectangle's top-left corner: X=0, Y=0 is the very top-left of the image; larger values move the corner right and down. Width and Height set the rectangle's size. Example: on a 2000×1500 photo, X=500, Y=300, Width=1000, Height=800 crops out a 1000×800 region starting 500 pixels from the left and 300 from the top.

Not sure of exact numbers? Start with the defaults, crop, and look at the result — then adjust. Since it's free and instant, trial and error costs nothing. As a shortcut for centering: to crop a WxH region from the middle of an image that's IW×IH pixels, use X=(IW−W)/2 and Y=(IH−H)/2. (The tool shows your image's dimensions in the preview area's context, and caps the fields at the image size.)

Tips for better crops

  • Crop, don't stretch. Cropping removes pixels; it never distorts. If the result looks "wrong," it's the region choice, not the tool.
  • Keep the original. Your source image is never modified — the crop is a new file. Keep the original in case you want a different crop later.
  • Mind the final size. A 200×200 crop from a large photo is fine for a thumbnail but will look blurry blown up. Crop generously if the result needs to display large.
  • Straighten first if needed. If the photo is tilted, fix rotation with the Image Rotator before cropping.
  • Need exact dimensions instead? To scale the whole image to specific pixel dimensions, use the Image Resizer.

Privacy: your photos stay on your device

Cropping happens locally in your browser — the image is never uploaded to any server. Cropped photos are often the personal ones (IDs, faces, private screenshots), so local processing is a genuine privacy win.

Honest limitations

  • Numeric entry, not drag-to-select. You type the X, Y, Width, and Height values rather than dragging a box on the preview. It's precise, but finding the right numbers takes a try or two.
  • Output is PNG. The cropped image always downloads as a PNG (name-cropped.png). For a smaller JPG, convert afterwards with the Image Converter.
  • Rectangular crops only. No circles, freehand shapes, or perspective correction — just clean rectangles.
  • One crop at a time. Each run produces one cropped image.

Frequently asked questions

Is cropping images online free?

Yes — free, unlimited, no signup, no watermarks.

Will cropping reduce quality?

No. Cropping cuts out pixels without recompressing or altering the rest — the kept region is pixel-identical to the original.

What do X and Y mean?

X is the distance in pixels from the left edge, Y from the top edge — together they set where your crop region's top-left corner sits.

Are my images uploaded anywhere?

No. Cropping happens entirely in your browser; your images never leave your device.
