How to Count Characters Online for Free (2026 Guide)

Character limits are everywhere: the 160-character SMS segment, social media bios, meta titles that get cut off in Google results, essay requirements, form fields that silently truncate your input. Guessing the length of your text is how you end up with a tweet that won't post or an ad headline chopped mid-word. Our Character Counter counts as you type — characters, characters without spaces, and lines — instantly and live. Free, no signup, everything in your browser.

What the character counter does

As soon as you type or paste text, the tool updates a live summary with three numbers:

  • Characters — the total count, including spaces and line breaks.
  • Without spaces — the count with all whitespace removed, which is what some forms and platforms use.
  • Lines — how many lines your text occupies.

There's no button to press and nothing to submit — the count refreshes with every keystroke, so you can watch the number change as you edit. If you need full writing stats (words, sentences, paragraphs, reading time), our Word Counter is the companion tool.

Step-by-step: counting characters

  1. Open the Character Counter.
  2. Type or paste your text into the box.
  3. Read the live line below it, e.g. 245 characters • 198 without spaces • 4 lines.
  4. Keep editing — the numbers update in real time as you type, delete or paste.

That's genuinely all there is to it. The counter starts working on an empty box (showing zero) and follows you all the way.

Example 1: fitting a social media bio

Platforms cap bios at tight lengths, and every character counts. Draft your bio — say, "Digital marketer helping small brands grow | DM for collabs" — paste it in, and watch the total. At 62 characters you have room to add a location or a link keyword; at 158 you need to trim two characters. Edit right in the box and the count updates as you go, so you can shave words until you land exactly where you want.

Example 2: SMS and messaging limits

A standard SMS segment holds 160 characters; longer messages are split (and billed) as multiple segments. Before sending a bulk message or an important one-off, paste the text into the counter. If it reads 172 characters, you know it will send as two segments — trimming 12 characters saves cost and keeps the message whole. The "without spaces" figure is also useful here, since some carriers and gateways count differently.

Example 3: SEO meta titles and descriptions

Google typically displays about 50–60 characters of a page title and roughly 155–160 of a meta description before truncating. Paste your draft title into the counter: if it reads 68 characters, expect the tail to be cut off in search results. Rewrite and re-paste until you're in range. Bloggers pairing this with the Word Counter get both length and readability stats for every post.

Example 4: essays and application forms

"Describe yourself in 500 characters or fewer" — forms love this constraint. Write freely, paste into the counter, and trim until the total is under the limit. Because the count is live, you can paste, edit, and watch the number fall in real time instead of guessing and re-submitting.

Practical tips

  • Know which count matters. Most platforms count total characters (with spaces); some forms and coding contexts use the no-spaces count. Check the rules of wherever you're submitting.
  • Watch out for invisible characters. Extra spaces and blank lines inflate the total — if the number looks wrong, run your text through our Whitespace Remover first.
  • Line breaks count too. Each new line adds to the total, which is why the tool shows the line count alongside.
  • Emoji count as characters. One emoji = one (or sometimes two) characters depending on the platform — don't assume they're free.

Example 5: coding and data limits

Developers hit character limits constantly: database columns defined as VARCHAR(255), API fields with max lengths, push notification payloads. Before wiring a value into code, paste it into the counter to confirm it fits — a 260-character string in a VARCHAR(255) column gets silently truncated and creates a debugging nightmare. The "without spaces" count also matters for systems that strip whitespace before validating length, like some username and password rules.

Privacy and cost

Counting happens in JavaScript on your device — your text is never uploaded, stored or analyzed anywhere. The tool is completely free with no limits.

Frequently asked questions

Yes in the main "characters" figure. The tool also shows a "without spaces" count, so you can use whichever one your situation requires.

Do I need to click anything to count?

No. The counter updates live as you type or paste — there are no buttons and no waiting.

The Character Counter is a lightweight live counter focused on characters and lines. The Word Counter gives deeper writing statistics: words, sentences, paragraphs and estimated reading time.
