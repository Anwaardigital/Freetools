How to Reverse Text Online for Free (2026 Guide)

Reversed text shows up in more places than you'd think: puzzle games and riddles, fun social media posts, coding exercises, cryptography basics, and creative writing tricks. Doing it by hand for more than a word or two is painfully slow — which is why our Text Reverser exists. It flips text, words, or entire lines in one click — free, no signup, everything processed in your browser.

What the text reverser does

Paste text into the input box and choose one of three reversal modes:

  • Reverse Text — flips every character: "hello world" becomes "dlrow olleh".
  • Reverse Words — flips the order of the words (each word stays intact): "hello world" becomes "world hello".
  • Flip Lines — reverses the order of lines in multi-line text (each line stays intact). Perfect for lists.

The result appears in the output box, and a Copy Result button puts it on your clipboard.

Step-by-step: reversing text

  1. Open the Text Reverser.
  2. Type or paste your text into the top box.
  3. Click Reverse Text (or Reverse Words / Flip Lines).
  4. The reversed result appears in the box below — click Copy Result to use it anywhere.

Example 1: puzzles and party games

Making a quiz or a treasure hunt? Reversed clues are a classic. "The key is under the mat" → Reverse Text → "tam eht rednu si yek ehT". Hand that to players and let them decode it. For a word-order puzzle, use Reverse Words: "mat the under is key the" scrambles the sentence while keeping every word readable — a different, often harder challenge.

Example 2: flipping a list

You have a leaderboard or a numbered list and need it in reverse order — newest first, or a countdown. Paste the lines in, hit Flip Lines, and the whole list reverses instantly. For a 200-item list this saves serious manual work, and unlike dragging rows around in a spreadsheet, you can't accidentally drop a line.

Example 3: coding and interview practice

"Reverse a string" is one of the most common beginner programming exercises. Our tool lets you generate expected outputs instantly: write the input, click Reverse Text, and compare with what your code produces. It's also handy for testing palindrome checkers — feed in "racecar" and confirm the output is identical, or feed in a sentence to verify your function handles spaces correctly.

Example 4: creative writing and design

Writers use reversed text for mirror-writing effects, secret messages in stories, and social posts that stand out in a feed. Designers use it for mock "upside-down" layouts and playful graphics. Because the tool is instant, you can experiment: reverse it, tweak a word, reverse again, until it reads the way you want.

Practical tips

  • Pick the right mode. Character reversal for codes and puzzles; word reversal for scrambled sentences; line flipping for lists. The wrong mode gives a confusing result.
  • Reversal is its own undo. Reversing reversed text restores the original — so you can always recover the input by running it through again.
  • Watch mixed content. Numbers and punctuation reverse too ("Room 101" → "101 mooR"), which is usually what you want for puzzles but worth checking in serious uses.
  • For secret messages, remember this is obfuscation, not encryption — anyone can reverse it back. Fun, not secure.

Example 5: un-flipping a flipped list

Flip Lines is also handy in reverse: someone sends you a list in the wrong order (oldest first, but you need newest first) and re-sorting in a spreadsheet would destroy the associated data. Paste just the list column, Flip Lines, and paste it back. Because each line's content is untouched, nothing gets scrambled — only the order changes.

Beyond fun: serious uses of reversal

Reversal isn't only play. Data-entry teams use Flip Lines to reverse chronological exports. QA testers use Reverse Text to generate edge-case inputs (reversed strings expose bugs in text handling). Language learners use Reverse Words to practice sentence structure — unscrambling a reversed-words sentence back into correct order is a genuine grammar exercise. And writers testing anagrams or palindromic phrases start with a reversal to see the raw material.

Privacy and cost

The reversal happens in JavaScript on your device — your text never leaves your browser. Free forever, no limits, no account needed.

Frequently asked questions

What's the difference between Reverse Text and Reverse Words?

Reverse Text flips every character ("hello" → "olleh"). Reverse Words keeps each word intact but flips their order ("hello world" → "world hello").

Can I reverse a whole list at once?

Yes — use Flip Lines. It reverses the order of the lines while leaving each line's content unchanged.

Is my text sent to a server?

No. All processing happens locally in your browser; nothing you enter is uploaded or stored.
