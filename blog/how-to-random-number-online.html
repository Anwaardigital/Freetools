How to Generate Random Numbers Online for Free (2026 Guide)

Picking a raffle winner, drawing lots, generating test data, sampling survey respondents — all of these need random numbers you can trust. Humans are notoriously bad at being random (we avoid repeats and favor certain digits), so a proper generator does it better. This guide shows you how to generate random numbers online for free, step by step, using a tool built on your browser's cryptographic random source.

Step-by-step: generate random numbers with our free tool

Our Random Number Generator runs entirely in your browser and uses crypto.getRandomValues — the operating system's cryptographic random source, the same one behind secure password generation. Here's how to use it:

  1. Open the Random Number Generator. You'll see three fields: Min, Max and Count.
  2. Set Min and Max — the range you want numbers from. Defaults are 1 to 100; change them to anything you like (e.g. 1 to 1000).
  3. Set Count — how many numbers to generate, from 1 up to 1,000.
  4. Tick "Unique numbers" if you need no repeats — essential for draws and lotteries where each winner must be different.
  5. Click "Generate". Your numbers appear instantly, separated by commas.

If you ask for more unique numbers than the range contains (e.g. 200 unique numbers from 1–100), the tool tells you plainly: "Range too small" — widen the range or lower the count.

Practical example: picking raffle winners

Your school fundraiser sold 200 numbered tickets and you need 5 winners. Here's the fair way:

  1. Set Min: 1, Max: 200, Count: 5.
  2. Tick Unique numbers — no ticket can win twice.
  3. Click Generate. You might get: 47, 133, 12, 198, 89.
  4. Announce tickets 47, 133, 12, 198 and 89 as winners — in the order drawn, or sorted, as you prefer.

Because the numbers come from a cryptographic source and the draw happens live where everyone can see the screen, the process is transparent and defensible.

Practical example: random sampling for a survey

You have a customer list of 1,500 people and want to survey a random sample of 50. Number your list 1–1,500, then generate Min: 1, Max: 1500, Count: 50 with Unique numbers ticked. The 50 numbers are your sample — every customer had an equal chance of selection, which is the definition of a fair random sample.

Practical example: test data in bulk

Testing a form that accepts ages 18–99? Generate Min: 18, Max: 99, Count: 100 (repeats allowed) and paste the results into your test script. Need 1,000 unique order numbers? Set the range wide enough (e.g. 1–999999) and generate 1,000 unique values in one click.

Real use cases

  • Raffles and giveaways: draw unique winners from numbered entries, live and transparently.
  • Classroom picks: number students and let the generator choose who answers — no accusations of favoritism.
  • Games and draws: lottery-style picks, secret-Santa assignment numbers, draft orders.
  • Statistics and research: random sampling, randomized trial group assignment.
  • Software testing: bulk numeric test data, randomized inputs for fuzzing small utilities.
  • Passwords and PINs (with care): the numbers are cryptographically random, so they're fine as components — but for actual passwords, use the dedicated Password Generator, which handles character sets properly.

Why "cryptographic" randomness matters

Not all random is equal. Simple pseudo-random generators (like the one behind most coin flips and dice apps) are fine for games but theoretically predictable. This tool uses crypto.getRandomValues, which draws from the operating system's entropy pool — unpredictable even in principle. For draws, sampling and anything where fairness could be questioned, that stronger guarantee is worth having.

Fair-draw checklist

  1. Number every entry from 1 to N with no gaps or duplicates.
  2. Set Max to exactly N — every entry must be reachable.
  3. Always tick "Unique numbers" for winner draws.
  4. Generate live where participants can watch, or record your screen.
  5. Publish the results — the drawn numbers and what they correspond to.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Forgetting "Unique numbers": without it, the same ticket can be drawn twice — fine for dice-like use, wrong for raffles.
  • Off-by-one ranges: 200 tickets numbered 1–200 means Max: 200, not 199 and not 201.
  • Using it for regulated gambling: this is a fair casual tool, not a certified lottery system. Real-money gambling needs licensed, audited infrastructure.
  • Confusing random with "evenly spread": true randomness includes clusters and repeats (when repeats are allowed). A "random-looking" hand-picked list is usually less random than the generator's output.
