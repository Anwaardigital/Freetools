How to Extract Text from PDFs Online for Free (2026 Guide)

You need a quote from a 40-page report. The contract clause buried on page 9. The references from a research paper. Retyping is out of the question — and copy-pasting from some PDF readers mangles everything into one unreadable block. Extracting a PDF's text properly gives you clean, usable text you can search, quote, translate, or paste into a document. Here's how to do it online, for free, in your browser.

When extracting text beats copy-paste

  • Long documents: grabbing all 40 pages at once instead of selecting screen by screen.
  • Quoting and citing: clean text for essays, articles, and reports.
  • Translation: paste extracted text into a translator instead of retyping.
  • Archiving: save a plain-text version that's tiny, searchable, and readable forever.
  • Accessibility: plain text works with screen readers and text-to-speech tools.

What you'll need

Any modern browser, desktop or mobile. No installation, no account. You need an internet connection the first time so the tool can load its PDF text engine; extraction itself then runs entirely on your device.

Step-by-step: extract text from a PDF

  1. Open the PDF to Text tool. You'll see a file selection area and an empty text box.
  2. Select your PDF file. Extraction starts automatically — you'll see an "Extracting text..." message.
  3. Wait for the text to appear. The tool reads the PDF page by page and fills the text box. Each page's text is clearly marked with a separator like --- Page 1 ---, --- Page 2 ---, so you always know where each passage came from.
  4. Copy or download:
    • "Copy Text" copies everything to your clipboard for pasting into a document, email, or translator.
    • "Download .txt" saves the full text as extracted.txt — a tiny plain-text file you can keep, search, and open anywhere.

Getting the best results

  • Use the page markers. The --- Page N --- separators make it easy to find where a quote came from — keep them if you're citing sources.
  • Expect plain text, not formatting. Bold, italics, font sizes, and multi-column layouts don't survive extraction. Columns may read top-to-bottom across the page rather than column-by-column. A quick cleanup in a text editor fixes most of this.
  • Search within the result. Once extracted, use your browser's find (Ctrl+F / Cmd+F) on the text box or in the downloaded .txt file to jump straight to keywords.
  • Large PDFs take a little longer. A 200-page document extracts page by page — give it a moment rather than clicking around.

What to do with extracted text

Once you have the text, a few workflows become easy. Quoting: paste passages into your document and use the page markers to add accurate citations. Translating: drop the text into any translation tool instead of retyping pages by hand. Searching: save the .txt file and use your operating system's file search to find phrases across many documents at once. Summarizing: feed long extractions into an AI summarizer to get the gist of a lengthy report in seconds. And archiving: a plain .txt file is a fraction of the PDF's size and will still open on any device decades from now — a practical backup of the words that matter.

Privacy: your documents stay private

Extraction happens locally in your browser — the PDF is never uploaded anywhere. That's especially valuable here, because the documents people extract text from are often sensitive: contracts, medical reports, legal filings, unpublished manuscripts. Browser-local extraction keeps them that way.

Honest limitations

  • Scanned PDFs have no text to extract. This is the big one: if a PDF is just photos of pages (common with older scans), there's no embedded text, and this tool won't invent any — it can't perform OCR. You'll get little or nothing back. Real text-based PDFs work perfectly.
  • Complex layouts get simplified. Tables, footnotes, and multi-column pages extract as running text; the reading order may need tidying.
  • Password-protected PDFs generally can't be processed until unlocked.
  • Images, charts, and diagrams don't convert to text — only actual text content is extracted.

If your PDF is a scan, the honest fix is OCR software (many free options exist) — no text-extraction tool can recover text that was never there.

Frequently asked questions

Is PDF to text conversion really free?

Yes — free, unlimited, no signup, no watermarks.

Why is my extracted text empty?

Almost certainly a scanned, image-only PDF. The pages are pictures, not text. You'd need OCR software to recognize the text in the images.

Will formatting be preserved?

No — you get plain text. Headings, bold, tables, and columns are flattened. It's meant for reading, quoting, searching, and translating, not for reproducing the layout.

Are my files uploaded anywhere?

No. Everything runs in your browser; your PDF never leaves your device.
