How to Convert HEIC Photos to JPG Online Free (2026 Guide)

You take a photo on your iPhone, send it to your computer or upload it to a website — and suddenly it will not open. The file ends in .heic, the website rejects it, and your friend on an older phone cannot see it at all. That is the HEIC problem, and it is one of the most common photo frustrations iPhone owners face. The good news is that fixing it takes less than a minute: convert the HEIC photo to JPG, the format every device, website and app understands. This guide shows you how to convert HEIC photos to JPG online for free, with no app to install and no account to create — plus how to stop your iPhone saving HEIC in the first place, how to convert a whole batch at once, and what to do with the JPG once you have it.

What is a HEIC file?

HEIC (High Efficiency Image Container) is the photo format Apple has used as the default on iPhones since iOS 11. It stores photos in smaller files than JPG at the same quality, which saves storage space on your phone. A HEIC file can also hold several images, depth data and live-photo frames in a single file, which is why Apple likes it. The format itself is good — the problem is compatibility. iPhones, iPads and newer Macs open HEIC files without thinking about it. Most Windows computers, older Android phones, many websites, email forms and online services do not. They expect JPG, and when they receive a HEIC file they either show an error or refuse the upload entirely.

Why convert HEIC to JPG?

  • JPG works everywhere: Every phone, computer, browser, printer, social network and website accepts JPG. It is the one photo format you never have to think about.
  • Upload forms accept it: Job applications, government portals, school forms, ID uploads and e-commerce listings almost always ask for JPG or PNG. A HEIC file is usually rejected on the spot.
  • Sharing becomes simple: Send a JPG to anyone and they can open it, whatever phone or computer they use. Send a HEIC file and you may spend the next ten minutes explaining why it will not open.
  • Editing tools expect it: Most online editors, design tools and photo apps work with JPG first. Converting first saves you from hitting a "format not supported" error halfway through your work.
  • Printing and backups: Print shops and older backup software handle JPG without any extra steps. HEIC often needs converting at the counter, which wastes your time.

JPG does have one weakness: it is a lossy format, which means each save throws away a little image data. That is why you should always keep your original HEIC file and convert a copy — never convert the only copy of a photo you care about, and never convert a JPG back and forth between formats repeatedly.

Method: convert HEIC to JPG online in 5 steps

The fastest way to convert one or a few photos is a free online HEIC to JPG converter. The whole job takes under a minute per photo:

  1. Pick a converter that asks for nothing: Use a free online converter that works in your browser without asking you to create an account, install an app, or hand over an email address. If a site demands a signup just to convert one photo, close it and use another one.
  2. Upload your HEIC photo: Drag the .heic file onto the page or tap the upload button and choose it from your phone or computer. Most converters accept several files at once if you want to convert a small batch together.
  3. Choose JPG and set the quality: Make sure JPG (sometimes written as JPEG) is the output format. If the converter offers a quality slider, keep it high — 90% or above — so the converted photo looks the same as the original. Lower quality makes a smaller file but a visibly worse photo.
  4. Convert and download: Tap Convert, wait a few seconds, then download the new .jpg file. Check that it opens on your device before you delete anything.
  5. Keep the original: Do not delete the HEIC file until you have opened the JPG, checked it looks right, and saved it where you want it. The original is your backup if anything goes wrong.

One warning: most online converters upload your photo to a server to do the conversion. That is fine for holiday photos and screenshots, but think twice before uploading private or sensitive photos — identity documents, family photos, or anything you would not want on someone else's server. For sensitive images, use the built-in conversion on your own phone or computer below instead, so the photo never leaves your device.

Convert HEIC to JPG without any website

If you would rather not upload your photos anywhere, your own devices can do the conversion for free:

On iPhone: stop saving HEIC for future photos

Open Settings, go to Camera, then Formats, and choose Most Compatible instead of High Efficiency. From then on your iPhone saves new photos as JPG. This does not change the HEIC photos already on your phone, but it stops the problem from coming back. It does use more storage space per photo, so if your phone is nearly full, keep High Efficiency on and convert only the photos you need to share.

On Mac: use Preview

Open the HEIC photo in Preview, click File, then Export, and choose JPEG as the format. Set the quality slider high and save. Preview can also convert several photos at once: select them all in the Finder, open them together, and export each one the same way.

On Windows: install the free HEIF extension

Windows does not open HEIC files out of the box. Install the free HEIF Image Extensions from the Microsoft Store, and the Photos app will open your HEIC files. From there you can save a copy as JPG. Without the extension, Windows will show an error and offer you a paid codec — you do not need to pay for it; the free extension from Microsoft is enough.

From iCloud: download as JPG

If your photos are in iCloud, open iCloud.com in a browser, select the photos you want, and download them. Photos downloaded from iCloud.com usually arrive as JPG files, which is a quick way to get shareable copies without installing anything.

Converting a whole batch of HEIC photos

Converting one photo is easy. Converting two hundred photos from a holiday needs a plan, or you will spend the whole evening clicking:

  • Do it in groups of 20 to 30: Most free online converters handle a small batch at once. Uploading hundreds of files in one go can time out or crash the page. Smaller groups are faster and safer.
  • Name the batch before you start: Put all the HEIC files for one event in one folder, convert them together, and save the JPGs into a folder with the same name plus "-jpg". You will thank yourself later when you are looking for them.
  • Check three photos from each batch: Open the first, one from the middle, and the last JPG of every batch and look at them properly. If the quality setting was wrong, you want to find out after three photos, not after two hundred.
  • Keep the originals in a separate folder: Move the HEIC files to a backup folder or an external drive instead of deleting them. Storage is cheap; a lost original photo is gone forever.
  • Convert only what you need: You do not have to convert your entire photo library. Convert the photos you actually want to share, print or upload, and leave the rest as HEIC on your phone where they take up less space.

Quality tips: keep your converted photos looking good

  • Always convert from the original HEIC: Converting a JPG that was already converted from a HEIC throws away quality twice. If you need another size or format later, go back to the original HEIC file and convert again.
  • Use quality 90% or higher: Below 80% you can usually see the damage — blocky patches in skies and skin, fuzzy edges, washed-out colours. For photos you care about, 90 to 95% is the sweet spot between quality and file size.
  • Do not upscale after converting: A converted JPG is at most as sharp as the original photo. Enlarging it afterwards only makes it blurry. If you need a bigger image, start from the highest-resolution original you have.
  • Check the colours: HEIC and JPG handle colour slightly differently, so open the converted JPG and compare it with the original on your phone. In most cases they look identical, but a quick check costs five seconds and catches the rare bad conversion.
  • Watch out for Live Photos: A Live Photo is a HEIC image plus a short video. Converting it to JPG keeps only the still image — the motion part is lost. If the motion matters, keep the original Live Photo and share it through AirDrop or iCloud instead.

Privacy: think before you upload a photo

A photo can reveal far more than the picture itself. Every photo carries hidden data — the date, the place it was taken, and the phone model — and a converter site receives all of it when you upload. For everyday photos that is a small risk, but follow these rules:

  • Never upload identity documents: Do not convert photos of your ID card, passport or driving licence on a random converter site. Use your phone or computer to convert those, so the image never leaves your device.
  • Prefer converters that delete your files: A trustworthy converter says clearly that it deletes uploaded files, usually within an hour. If a site says nothing about deletion, treat your photos as if they stay on its server.
  • Avoid converters that demand an account: Converting a photo should not need your email address or a password. A site that demands a signup for a one-minute job is collecting your data, not helping you.
  • Use browser-based tools when you can: The tools in this guide run entirely in your browser, on your own device — your photos are never uploaded anywhere. The Image Converter, Image Compressor and Image Resizer all work this way, which is why they are safe for every photo, including private ones.

Frequently asked questions

What is a HEIC file?

HEIC is the photo format iPhones have used by default since iOS 11. It stores high-quality photos in smaller files than JPG, but many Windows computers, websites and older phones cannot open it, which is why converting to JPG is so often needed.

Is it free to convert HEIC to JPG?

Yes. Free online converters handle single photos and small batches at no cost, and the built-in options on iPhone, Mac and Windows are free too. You should never need to pay or create an account just to convert a photo.

Will converting HEIC to JPG lose quality?

A little, because JPG is a lossy format — but at 90% quality or higher the difference is invisible in normal viewing. Always convert from the original HEIC file, never from an already-converted JPG, and keep the original as a backup.

Can I convert HEIC to JPG on my iPhone without a computer?

Yes. Change Settings, then Camera, then Formats to Most Compatible so future photos save as JPG, or use a free online converter in Safari. For photos already on your phone, downloading them from iCloud.com usually gives you JPG copies too.

How do I open a HEIC file on Windows?

Install the free HEIF Image Extensions from the Microsoft Store. After that, the Photos app opens HEIC files and you can save a copy as JPG. Do not pay for a codec — the free extension from Microsoft is all you need.

What should I do with the JPG after converting?

Most JPGs benefit from one more step: compress them for upload forms with the Image Compressor, resize them with the Image Resizer, or crop them with the Image Cropper. All three are free in your browser, with no signup.
