How to Create a Professional Invoice Online for Free (2026 Guide)

Whether you freelance, run a small shop, or just sold a service to one client, a professional invoice is what turns finished work into paid work. Clients pay faster when the invoice looks serious: clear totals, a due date, and payment details they do not have to ask about. The good news is that you do not need paid software or a template hunt — you can create a polished, professional invoice online for free, in your browser, in a few minutes. This guide shows you exactly what to include, how to calculate everything correctly, and how to send it so it gets paid.

What every professional invoice must include

Before you touch any tool, gather these details. An invoice missing any of them invites delays, questions, or disputes:

  • Your business details: your name or business name, address, email and phone number.
  • Your client's details: their name or business name and billing address.
  • Invoice number: a unique number for every invoice (for example INV-2026-001, then INV-2026-002). Never reuse numbers — they are how you and your client track payments.
  • Dates: the invoice date and a clear payment due date.
  • Line items: each product or service, the quantity, the rate, and the line total.
  • Totals: subtotal, any tax or discount, and the final amount due.
  • Payment instructions: exactly how to pay — bank transfer details, a payment link, or another method you accept.
  • Notes: payment terms (for example "Payment due within 14 days"), late-fee policy if you have one, and a short thank-you line.

Step 2: Get the numbers right — tax, discounts and totals

Arithmetic mistakes are the most common reason clients push back on an invoice. Double-check every line, and use the right tool for each calculation:

  • Tax: if you charge sales tax or VAT, work it out with the VAT Calculator so the tax line is exact, not guessed. Show the tax rate and the tax amount as separate lines — clients and accountants both want to see them.
  • Discounts: if you offer a percentage discount, check it with the Percentage Calculator before you put it on the invoice. A discount that does not add up undermines trust instantly.
  • Foreign clients: invoicing in another currency? Convert the totals with the Currency Converter and state the currency clearly on the invoice (for example "USD 450" rather than just "450").
  • Due dates: a due date like "within 14 days" is vague once the invoice sits in an inbox. Put an actual calendar date on it, and if you count working days, verify the span with the Date Duration Calculator.

Step 3: Make it look professional

Clients judge an invoice the way they judge any business document — in seconds. A few habits make yours look like it came from a much bigger company:

  • One page if possible: most invoices fit on one page. If yours runs longer, keep the totals on the final page and number the pages.
  • Consistent branding: use the same business name, email and styling on every invoice. Clients should recognize your invoices at a glance.
  • Clear amounts: the amount due should be the biggest, easiest number to find. Never make the client hunt for what they owe.
  • No typos in names or amounts: read the invoice once, out loud, before sending. A misspelled client name or a wrong total is the fastest way to look careless.
  • PDF, not a photo or screenshot: a PDF looks professional, prints cleanly and cannot be accidentally edited in transit.

Step 4: Send it the right way

How you send the invoice matters almost as much as what is on it. Email remains the standard: attach the PDF, and write a short message in the email body — what the invoice covers, the amount due, and the due date. Do not send the invoice as a bare attachment with no message, and do not paste the whole invoice into the email as an image.

If the client does not pay by the due date, follow up politely and promptly: a short reminder on the due date, then again a week later. Most late payments are not refusals — they are forgotten inboxes. A calm, factual reminder ("Invoice INV-2026-001 for USD 450 was due on 5 November") gets paid faster than an angry one. Keep every invoice and every reminder in one folder so you always know who owes what.

Common invoice mistakes to avoid

  • No invoice number: without one, neither you nor the client can refer to the payment unambiguously.
  • No due date: "pay when you can" means "never" in practice. Always set a date.
  • Vague line items: "Services — 500" invites questions. "Website homepage design — 10 hours at 50 per hour" does not.
  • Missing payment details: if the client has to email you to ask how to pay, you have already delayed your own payment.
  • Sending a draft: never send an invoice you have not re-read. One wrong digit can take weeks to untangle.

A simple invoice checklist

Before you hit send, run through this list: your details correct, client details correct, unique invoice number, invoice date and due date, clear line items, subtotal plus tax minus discount equals the total, payment instructions included, PDF attached, email message written. If every box is ticked, your invoice is ready.
