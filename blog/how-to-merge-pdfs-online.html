How to Merge PDFs Online for Free (2026 Guide)

Job application with a cover letter, CV and certificates. A stack of monthly invoices. Chapters of a report scattered across separate files. Sooner or later, everyone needs to combine several PDFs into a single document — and it shouldn't require installing software, creating an account, or uploading sensitive files to a stranger's server. This guide shows you how to merge PDFs online for free, in your browser, in about a minute.

Why merge PDFs?

A single combined PDF is easier to send, easier to file, and harder to lose a piece of. Common situations:

  • Job applications: cover letter + CV + certificates as one file, in the order the employer expects.
  • Expense reports: a month of receipts merged into one document.
  • Legal and official paperwork: forms and supporting documents as one bundle.
  • Study notes: lecture slides and handouts combined into one revision file.

What you'll need

Just a modern browser — Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari, or the browser on your phone. Nothing to install, no account to create. You will need an internet connection the first time you use the tool, because the PDF engine loads once from a CDN; after that, the actual merging happens entirely on your device.

Step-by-step: merge PDFs online

  1. Open the PDF Merger. You'll see a file selection area labeled "Select 2+ PDF files to merge (in order)".
  2. Select your PDF files — in the order you want them merged. Hold Ctrl (or Cmd on Mac) to select multiple files at once. The first file you select becomes the first pages of the combined document, the second becomes the next pages, and so on.
  3. Check the file list. After selecting, the tool shows a numbered list of your files with each file's size — e.g. "1. cover-letter.pdf (84 KB)". Verify the order is exactly what you want.
  4. Click "Merge PDFs". The tool combines every page of every file, in order. You'll briefly see a "Merging..." message.
  5. Download the result. The combined file downloads automatically as merged.pdf, and the tool reports its size. Open it to confirm everything is in the right order, then rename it to something meaningful.

Getting the order right

Order is the one thing people get wrong most often. The tool merges files in the exact order you select them, so a little preparation saves rework:

  • Rename files before merging with a number prefix — 01-cover-letter.pdf, 02-cv.pdf, 03-certificates.pdf — then sort by name in the file dialog.
  • Select all files in one go rather than adding them one at a time, so the order is predictable.
  • Double-check the numbered list before clicking Merge. If the order is wrong, simply re-select the files in the correct order.

Tips for a clean merge

  • Check page sizes: merging a mix of A4 and Letter pages is fine — each page keeps its original size — but for a uniform look, keep source pages the same size.
  • Keep the originals: the tool creates a new file and never modifies your originals, but it's good practice to keep them until you've verified the merged result.
  • Need to pull pages out instead? Use the PDF Splitter to extract specific pages or ranges from a PDF.
  • File too big to email after merging? Run the result through the PDF Compressor to shrink it.

Privacy: where do your files go?

Nowhere. The merge happens locally in your browser using a client-side PDF library. Your files are never uploaded to any server, never stored, and never seen by anyone but you. This makes browser-based merging a genuinely safer option than upload-and-merge services, especially for contracts, IDs, bank statements, or anything confidential.

Honest limitations

No tool is perfect, so here's what to expect:

  • Password-protected PDFs: encrypted files generally can't be merged until unlocked. Remove the password first (if you own the file), then merge.
  • Very large files: merging works in your device's memory, so extremely large PDFs (hundreds of megabytes) may be slow or fail on low-memory phones. Merge in smaller batches if needed.
  • Interactive elements: pages merge faithfully, but form fields, embedded videos, and some bookmarks may not carry over into the combined file.
  • At least two files required: if you select only one file, the tool will ask you to select at least two.

Frequently asked questions

Is merging PDFs online really free?

Yes — this tool is free with no limits, no signup, no watermarks, and no file-count caps beyond what your browser's memory can handle.

Does merging reduce quality?

No. Pages are copied into the new document, not re-rendered or re-compressed, so text and images keep their original quality.

Can I merge PDFs on my phone?

Yes. The tool works in mobile browsers. Select the PDFs from your files app (or Google Drive/Files), merge, and the result downloads to your phone.

Are my documents uploaded anywhere?

No. All processing happens locally in your browser. The only network request is loading the PDF engine the first time; your files never leave your device.
