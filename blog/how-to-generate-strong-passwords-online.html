How to Generate Strong Passwords Online for Free (2026 Guide)

Most passwords people invent are terrible — and understandably so. Human brains are built for stories, not randomness, so we reuse the same pet name with a number tacked on the end. Attackers know this, and their guessing tools are built to exploit exactly those habits. The fix is simple: stop inventing passwords and start generating them. A good password generator creates passwords no human could guess, in seconds, for free. This guide shows you how to generate strong passwords online the right way — how long they should be, what "random" really means, the mistakes to avoid, and a private workflow that never sends your passwords anywhere.

What makes a password strong?

Password strength comes down to two things: length and unpredictability. Length matters most because every extra character multiplies the number of combinations an attacker must try. A 12-character random password has roughly 9512 possibilities — far beyond what any guessing attack can chew through. Unpredictability matters because attackers don't guess randomly: they try dictionary words, common patterns ("qwerty", "123456", birthdays), and passwords leaked in previous breaches, in order of likelihood.

  • Length: aim for at least 16 characters for important accounts (email, banking, password manager). 20+ is even better.
  • Character variety: uppercase, lowercase, digits and symbols each add possibilities — but only if they're placed randomly, not as predictable decorations (adding "!" at the end of a word fools nobody).
  • Uniqueness: every account gets its own password. One reused password turns a single breached website into the key to your email, your bank and your social accounts.

The passphrase alternative

If you need a password you can actually memorize — for your password manager's master password, for example — consider a passphrase: five or six random words strung together, like "correct horse battery staple" done properly with genuinely random word selection. A six-word passphrase from a large word list has enormous strength and is far easier to type and remember than 20 random characters. Our generator focuses on random passwords; for a master passphrase, roll physical dice against a word list (the classic Diceware method) so even your device never sees the process.

5 mistakes that undo strong passwords

  • Reusing one "strong" password everywhere. Strength means nothing if one breach leaks it. Use our Password Generator to make a fresh one per account.
  • Storing passwords in notes or spreadsheets. Use a reputable password manager instead — it remembers and autofills them for you.
  • Skipping two-factor authentication. Even a perfect password can be phished. Turn on 2FA (preferably an authenticator app, not SMS) on email, banking and social accounts.
  • Answering "security questions" honestly. Your mother's maiden name and first pet are public information. Treat security answers as second passwords and generate random ones.
  • Changing passwords on a schedule. Forced 90-day changes make people pick weaker passwords. Change a password when there's a reason — a breach, a phishing scare, a shared computer — not on a timer.

Frequently asked questions

Are online password generators safe?

Only if the generation happens in your browser with no data sent anywhere. Ours works exactly that way — open the page, disconnect your internet if you want to prove it to yourself, and it still generates passwords.

How long should a password be in 2026?

16 characters minimum for important accounts, 20+ where allowed. Shorter passwords (12) are acceptable for low-value accounts you'll rarely use.

Is it safe to store passwords in my browser?

Browser password managers are better than reusing passwords, but a dedicated password manager with a strong master password and 2FA is the safer choice.

What if a site limits password length?

Some older sites cap passwords at 12–16 characters. Use the maximum length they allow, and make sure it's still unique to that site.
