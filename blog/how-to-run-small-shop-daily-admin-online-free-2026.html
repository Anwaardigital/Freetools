How to Run a Small Shop's Daily Admin Online for Free (2026 Guide)

Running a small shop means dozens of small admin jobs every day: writing an invoice, pricing a new product, making a label, converting a supplier's price, working out a discount. None of them is hard, but together they eat hours — and paid business software is often overkill for a shop with a handful of products and a handful of customers a day.

The good news is that every one of these jobs can be done in a browser, for free, with no signup and nothing to install. This guide builds a simple daily admin routine around free online tools, so the paperwork stops piling up and the numbers stay correct.

The daily routine: 15 minutes, morning and evening

Admin works best as a habit, not a monthly panic. Try this shape: in the morning, prepare the day's prices and labels; in the evening, record the day's sales and invoices. Fifteen minutes at each end of the day keeps everything current, and you never have to reconstruct a week's sales from memory.

1. Send professional invoices in minutes

A handwritten invoice is fine until a customer asks for a copy, a tax record or a payment reminder. A typed invoice with your shop name, the items, quantities, prices and total looks professional and is much easier to find again later. Use our free Invoice Generator to fill in your details, add line items, and download the invoice as a PDF you can print, email or send on WhatsApp. Save one copy per customer in a single folder, named by date, and you have a sales record without any extra work.

Always include the date, a simple invoice number (even 001, 002, 003 works), the payment method, and whether the invoice is paid or pending. Those four details answer almost every question a customer or accountant will ever ask.

2. Price labels customers can scan

Printed price tags are easy to lose and hard to update. A QR code on a shelf label can point customers to a product page, a price list or your WhatsApp catalogue, and you can reprint it whenever the price changes. Generate it free with our QR Code Generator, print it at a decent size with a clear call to action like "Scan for price", and test it with two different phones before printing a whole batch.

For stock you track yourself, a barcode is even better. Our Barcode Generator creates standard barcodes you can stick on products or bins, so counting stock becomes scanning instead of guessing. Keep a simple list matching each barcode to a product name and price — a plain document is enough at this scale.

3. Convert supplier prices correctly

Suppliers often quote in another currency, and a wrong conversion quietly eats your margin. Before ordering, convert the quote with our Currency Converter, add shipping and any taxes, and only then set your selling price. Write the converted cost next to the product in your list, so next month's order uses the same maths instead of a new guess.

As a rule, never set a selling price from the foreign-currency figure alone. Convert first, add costs second, add margin last — in that order, every time.

4. Discounts without margin mistakes

Discounts are where small shops lose money without noticing. A "20% off" sign is easy to write and easy to get wrong at the till, especially on items with different costs. Before a sale, check the final price with our Discount Calculator: enter the original price and the discount, and it gives you the exact sale price and the amount saved. Print the sale price on the label so staff and customers see the same number.

Set one rule for yourself: never discount below your cost plus a minimum margin, and write that floor price on your product list. If a discount would cross it, the answer is a smaller discount or a bundle deal instead.

5. Write product descriptions that sell

Online listings, WhatsApp catalogues and social media posts all need short, clear product descriptions. Keep them under a few sentences, lead with the benefit, and include size, material and price. Use our Word Counter to check length before posting, so descriptions fit the platform instead of getting cut off mid-sentence.

One good photo, one honest description and the correct price will outsell ten vague posts. Customers trust shops whose listings match what actually arrives.

6. Keep receipts and records tidy

At the end of the week, gather the week's invoices and receipts into one PDF with our PDF Merger. One file per week, stored in a single folder, is a record system you can actually maintain. If a customer disputes a payment or you need last month's totals, you open one file instead of searching through a drawer.

Because all of these tools run in your browser, nothing is uploaded to a server: your prices, customer names and invoices stay on your own device. That privacy matters for a small business — your supplier prices and margins are your business, not a software company's data.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to sign up for any of these tools?

No. Every tool linked in this guide is free and works without an account, so you can start using them in the next five minutes.

Can I use these tools on my phone?

Yes. They all run in a mobile browser, which is handy for making a label or an invoice at the shop counter.

What if my shop grows beyond this routine?

That is a good problem. Keep this routine until it genuinely stops fitting — usually when you have staff doing admin or hundreds of products — and only then look at paid software, with your existing records ready to import.
