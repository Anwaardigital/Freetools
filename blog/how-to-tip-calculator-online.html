How to Calculate Tips and Split Bills Online for Free (2026 Guide)

The bill arrives, four friends look at each other, and someone starts doing long division on a napkin. A free online tip calculator ends that ritual: enter the bill, set the tip percentage, say how many people are splitting — and you get the tip, the total and each person's share instantly. This guide walks through the exact steps, works two examples, and covers the honest etiquette of when (and when not) to tip.

Step 1: Open the Tip Calculator

Open our Tip Calculator. Free, no signup, works entirely in your browser — handy at the restaurant table where you just need an answer, not an app install.

Step 2: Enter the bill details

Three fields:

  • Bill amount — the total on the receipt, before tip (pre-filled with 50)
  • Tip % — your chosen tip percentage (pre-filled with 15)
  • People — how many are splitting the bill (pre-filled with 1)

Enter the bill exactly as printed. If tax is already included in the total, just use that total — the tool does straight percentage math on whatever you enter.

Step 3: Click "Calculate"

Press "Calculate" and you get three answers:

  • Tip — the tip amount in your currency
  • Total — bill + tip
  • Per person — the total divided equally among the group

Worked example 1: dinner for four

Bill = 1,200, tip = 15%, people = 4:

  1. Tip: 1,200 × 15 ÷ 100 = 180
  2. Total: 1,200 + 180 = 1,380
  3. Per person: 1,380 ÷ 4 = 345

Everyone owes 345 — clean numbers, no napkin math, no argument.

Worked example 2: quick lunch for two

Bill = 850, tip = 10%, people = 2:

  1. Tip: 850 × 10 ÷ 100 = 85
  2. Total: 850 + 85 = 935
  3. Per person: 935 ÷ 2 = 467.50

Notice the tool handles the .50 cleanly — the awkward remainders that make manual splitting annoying.

How much should you tip? (honest answer)

Tipping customs vary enormously by country and culture — there is no universal rule, and this tool doesn't pretend there is. Some honest context:

  • In countries like the US, 15–20% at restaurants is the common expectation; the tool's 15% default reflects that.
  • In much of Europe, service is often included — rounding up or 5–10% for great service is typical.
  • In Japan, South Korea and parts of China, tipping can be awkward or refused — check local norms before offering.
  • In many Middle Eastern, South Asian and African countries, a 10% service charge may already be on the bill — tipping on top is optional.

Always check the receipt first: if a service charge is already included, adding another tip means tipping twice. The calculator can't know — you can.

Fair ways to split (beyond equal shares)

The tool splits equally, which is right most of the time. For trickier tables:

  • Uneven orders: run the tool twice — once for the shared items (split by everyone), once for individual big-ticket items.
  • Someone didn't drink: calculate the food bill split for all, then split drinks only among drinkers.
  • Rounding for cash: round each person's share up to the nearest convenient note — the extra becomes part of the tip.

Tipping beyond restaurants

The same calculator works anywhere a percentage-based gratuity applies. Delivery drivers, taxi and rideshare trips, hotel housekeeping, haircuts, tour guides — the math is identical: enter the service cost, set your percentage, and (for groups) the headcount. A few norms worth knowing: food delivery tips often run 10–15% of the order; rideshare apps suggest similar ranges; hotel porters and housekeeping are usually tipped in flat amounts rather than percentages, so the calculator's job there is just splitting the total fairly. The key habit is the same everywhere: decide the amount deliberately instead of guessing under social pressure. A calculator removes the awkward pause and replaces it with a confident, fair number.

Tips for using the calculator at the table

  • Decide the tip % before opening the tool — the math is instant; the etiquette decision is the slow part.
  • Use the printed total, including tax, unless your group agreed otherwise.
  • Double-check the people count — the most common error is forgetting someone who stepped out.
  • Bookmark the tool — it's faster than any restaurant bill math you'll do by hand.

Frequently asked questions

Yes — free, unlimited, no signup, runs in your browser.

Does it work in my currency?

Yes — the tool does pure math on the numbers you enter, so rupees, dollars, euros and anything else all work identically.

Should I tip on the pre-tax or post-tax amount?

Custom varies; many people tip on the pre-tax subtotal. The tool uses whatever total you enter — if you want pre-tax, enter the subtotal instead.

What if one person wants to pay more?

Get the per-person baseline from the tool, then adjust: the generous friend adds their extra on top. The tool gives you the fair starting point.
