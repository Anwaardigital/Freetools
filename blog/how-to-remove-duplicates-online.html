How to Remove Duplicate Lines Online for Free (2026 Guide)

Merge two mailing lists and you'll get the same address twice. Export data from two systems and the overlap appears in both. Copy names from several sources and a few repeat. Duplicate lines are one of the most common data annoyances — and manually hunting them in a 500-line list is a miserable job. Our Remove Duplicate Lines tool deletes repeats in one click, keeping the first occurrence of each line. Free, no signup, everything in your browser.

What the tool does

Paste your lines (one per line) and click Remove Duplicates. The tool scans the list and keeps only the first occurrence of each unique line, preserving the original order of everything else. Two checkboxes control the matching, both on by default:

  • Case sensitive (on) — "Apple" and "apple" are treated as different lines. Turn it off to treat them as the same.
  • Trim whitespace (on) — leading and trailing spaces are ignored when comparing, so " apple" and "apple" count as duplicates, and the cleaned line is kept.

Two extra buttons come along for the ride: Sort A–Z (alphabetical sort) and Reverse Lines (flip the list order), plus Copy Result for the cleaned output.

Step-by-step: removing duplicates

  1. Open the Remove Duplicate Lines tool.
  2. Paste your list into the top box — one item per line.
  3. Check the options: leave Case sensitive and Trim whitespace on for most jobs, or turn off case sensitivity if "Apple" and "apple" should merge.
  4. Click Remove Duplicates.
  5. The clean list appears below — click Copy Result to use it.

Example 1: cleaning a mailing list

You combined signups from a website form and a paper sheet at an event:

ali@example.com
sara@example.com
ali@example.com
Ali@example.com
  sara@example.com

With both checkboxes on, Remove Duplicates gives you ali@example.com, sara@example.com, and Ali@example.com — because the capital-A version is technically different and the spaced version got trimmed and merged. For email work, turn off case sensitivity (email addresses are case-insensitive in practice) and you'll get exactly two clean addresses. That's the power of the option: match the tool's strictness to your data.

Example 2: keyword and tag lists

SEO keyword research produces overlapping lists from different tools. Paste them all in, dedupe, then hit Sort A–Z to get one clean alphabetical master list. Bloggers can do the same with tags: combine the tags from ten posts, remove duplicates, and you have a consistent tag list for the whole site instead of three near-identical variants of everything.

Example 3: survey and form responses

Exported a column of "city" answers from a survey? Dedup it to see the unique values, then Sort A–Z for a tidy summary. It's the fastest possible way to answer "which distinct values appear in this column?" without opening a spreadsheet.

When duplicates are tricky

  • Case variants: "New York" vs "new york" — turn off case sensitivity to merge them.
  • Stray spaces: entries copied from PDFs and web pages often carry invisible trailing spaces; the trim option catches these.
  • Near-duplicates aren't duplicates: "John Smith" and "Jon Smith" are different lines to the tool — it only removes exact matches, which is the safe behavior.
  • Order is preserved: the first occurrence stays where it was, so ranked or chronological lists keep their meaning.

Practical tips

  • Normalize first for messy data: run text through our Case Converter (lowercase) and Whitespace Remover before deduping for the most thorough cleanup.
  • Dedupe before counting: unique-value counts are meaningless until repeats are gone.
  • Sort after deduping, not before — Sort A–Z on the clean list gives you the final tidy version.
  • Check the count: compare line counts before and after to see how many duplicates were removed.

Example 4: cleaning exported data columns

Data exported from CRMs and spreadsheets often repeats values down a column — the same country, status or category on hundreds of rows. Paste the column into the tool, remove duplicates, and you instantly have the distinct values: perfect for building dropdown lists, filter options or pivot summaries. Analysts use this as a poor man's "unique values" function without opening Excel at all.

Privacy and cost

All processing happens in JavaScript on your device — your lists are never uploaded or stored. The tool is free forever with no limits.

Frequently asked questions

Does it keep the first or last occurrence?

The first occurrence is kept; later repeats are removed. The original order of the surviving lines is preserved.

What does "case sensitive" mean here?

With it on, "Apple" and "apple" are different lines. With it off, they're treated as duplicates and merged. Turn it off when your data treats case as irrelevant (like email addresses).

Can it sort the list too?

Yes — the Sort A–Z button alphabetizes the lines, and Reverse Lines flips their order. Both work on any text in the box, duplicates or not.
