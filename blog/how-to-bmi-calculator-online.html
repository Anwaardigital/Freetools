How to Calculate Your BMI Online for Free (2026 Guide)

Body Mass Index (BMI) is the quickest screening number for whether your weight is in a typical healthy range for your height. Doctors, gyms and insurance forms all use it — and you can compute yours in seconds with a free online tool. This guide gives you the exact steps, two worked examples, what each category means, and — just as important — what BMI can't tell you.

Honest disclaimer: BMI is a population-level screening estimate, not a diagnosis and not medical advice. It doesn't distinguish muscle from fat. If your result concerns you, talk to a doctor or dietitian rather than acting on a number alone.

Step 1: Open the BMI Calculator

Open our BMI Calculator. It runs entirely in your browser — your weight and height are never uploaded, which is exactly how health-adjacent data should be handled.

Step 2: Enter your weight and height

You'll see two fields:

  • Weight (kg) — pre-filled with 70
  • Height (cm) — pre-filled with 170

Replace these with your own numbers. For best accuracy, weigh yourself in the morning on a flat surface, and measure your height standing straight against a wall. If you know your height in feet/inches, convert first: 1 foot = 30.48 cm, 1 inch = 2.54 cm (or use our Unit Converter).

Step 3: Click "Calculate BMI"

Press "Calculate BMI" and the tool shows two things:

  • Your BMI number (e.g. 24.2)
  • Your category — color-coded: Underweight, Normal weight, Overweight or Obese

Worked example 1: 70 kg, 170 cm

The formula is: BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)²

  1. Convert height: 170 cm = 1.70 m
  2. Square it: 1.70 × 1.70 = 2.89
  3. Divide: 70 ÷ 2.89 = 24.2

24.2 falls in the Normal weight range (18.5–24.9). The tool shows this in green.

Worked example 2: 85 kg, 175 cm

  1. Height: 175 cm = 1.75 m; squared = 3.0625
  2. BMI: 85 ÷ 3.0625 = 27.8

27.8 falls in the Overweight range (25.0–29.9). The tool shows this in orange. Note: this doesn't automatically mean unhealthy — see the limits section below.

BMI categories explained

The standard adult categories the tool uses:

  • Below 18.5 — Underweight: may warrant checking nutrition with a professional.
  • 18.5–24.9 — Normal weight: the typical healthy range for most adults.
  • 25.0–29.9 — Overweight: a nudge to look at diet and activity; not a diagnosis.
  • 30.0 and above — Obese: associated with higher health risks at population level; worth discussing with a doctor.

These cutoffs are the widely used WHO/CDC adult thresholds — handy because every doctor and form uses the same scale.

The honest limits of BMI

BMI is useful but crude. It cannot tell muscle from fat, so athletes and manual workers can land in "Overweight" while being perfectly fit. It also ignores fat distribution (belly fat matters more than BMI alone suggests), age, sex and ethnicity — researchers have long noted the standard cutoffs fit some populations better than others. Treat your BMI as one data point, alongside waist measurement, activity level, blood pressure and how you actually feel.

Practical uses for a quick BMI check

  • Baseline tracking: re-check monthly under the same conditions (morning, same scale) to spot trends.
  • Fitness goal-setting: a concrete number makes "get healthier" measurable.
  • Medical forms: some pre-screening questionnaires ask for BMI.
  • Pairing with other numbers: combine with waist circumference for a fuller picture than either alone.

Tracking your BMI over time

A single BMI reading is a snapshot; the trend is the real story. If you check monthly and log the number, you can see whether lifestyle changes are actually moving the needle — far more motivating than one isolated figure. Keep conditions identical each time: same scale, same time of day, similar clothing. Small fluctuations of 0.2–0.3 are normal noise from hydration and meals, so look at the direction over 2–3 months, not week to week. And remember what the number can't see: if you've started strength training, the scale may stall or rise while your body composition improves — that's muscle, not a failure. Pair the trend with how your clothes fit, your energy levels and your waist measurement for a verdict BMI alone can't give.

Tips for accurate measurement

  • Weigh consistently: same time of day, similar clothing, same scale.
  • Measure height barefoot against a wall, standing tall — many people overestimate their height by a couple of centimeters, which artificially lowers BMI.
  • Not for kids: children's BMI is interpreted against age-and-sex percentile charts, not these adult cutoffs — this tool is for adults.
  • Not for pregnancy: pre-pregnancy weight plus gestational gain is tracked differently by doctors.

Frequently asked questions

Is the BMI calculator free?

Yes — free, unlimited, no signup. Everything is computed on your device.

What's a "good" BMI?

For most adults, 18.5–24.9 is the standard healthy range. But "good" depends on the individual — muscle mass, age and health history all matter.

Why kg and cm instead of pounds and inches?

The metric formula is simplest (kg ÷ m²). If you think in pounds and inches, convert first with our Unit Converter.

Can BMI diagnose obesity?

No. BMI is a screening tool. Diagnosis considers body composition, waist size, lab results and clinical judgment — that's a doctor's job, not a number's.
