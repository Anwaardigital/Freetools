How to Generate Hashtags for Instagram, TikTok and YouTube Free (2026 Guide)

You can post the best photo or video of your life and still get almost no views — because nobody found it. Hashtags are how strangers find your content. They are the labels that tell Instagram, TikTok and YouTube what your post is about, so the platforms can show it to people who care about that topic. Used well, a few good hashtags put your post in front of exactly the right audience. Used badly, they do nothing — or worse, they make your post look like spam. This guide shows you how to generate hashtags for Instagram, TikTok and YouTube for free, in your browser, with no app to install and no account to create — plus how many to use on each platform, how to find trending ones, and the mistakes that quietly kill your reach.

What hashtags actually do (and what they do not)

  • They categorise your post: When you add #homemadepizza to a cooking video, you are filing it under that topic. People who search for or follow that topic can find your post even if they have never heard of you.
  • They help the algorithm understand you: Platforms read your hashtags alongside your caption, image and sound to decide who might like your post. Clear, relevant hashtags give the algorithm a better signal than vague ones.
  • They build a community: Niche hashtags like #cricketfanspakistan or #handmadejewellery gather people who genuinely care about one thing. A small, active community beats a huge, uninterested audience every time.
  • They do not guarantee views: No hashtag can save a boring post, and no tool can promise a viral result. Hashtags open the door; your content still has to be worth watching once someone walks through it.
  • They are not a shortcut for spam: Stuffing thirty unrelated popular hashtags under a post does not trick the platforms. It usually does the opposite — your post gets shown to people who do not care, they scroll past, and the platform learns your post is not interesting.

How many hashtags should you use?

Every platform has its own limits and its own culture. The right number is always "enough to describe the post clearly, and no more". Here is what works in practice:

  • Instagram — 3 to 5 focused hashtags: Instagram allows up to 30 hashtags on a post, but posts with a small set of relevant tags usually perform better and look far cleaner. Pick three to five that truly describe your post: one broad topic, two or three niche ones, and one for your community or location. Put them at the end of your caption, or in the first comment if you prefer a tidy caption.
  • TikTok — 3 to 5 hashtags: TikTok captions are short, and hashtags compete for space with your words. Three to five is the sweet spot: one or two broad tags for the topic, one or two niche tags, and one trending tag if it genuinely fits your video. Never add a trending hashtag just because it is trending — if your video is not about that trend, viewers will skip it.
  • YouTube — up to 15 in the description, 3 shown above the title: YouTube lets you add up to 15 hashtags in a video description; if you add more than 15, YouTube ignores all of them. The first three appear as clickable links above your video title, so make those three count. YouTube also removes hashtags that break its rules, so keep them relevant and clean.
  • The golden rule for all three: Relevance beats quantity. Five hashtags that match your post will always outperform twenty random ones. If a hashtag does not describe what is actually in the post, leave it out.

The three types of hashtags every post needs

A good hashtag set is a mix, not a pile of the same kind. Think of it as three layers:

1. Broad hashtags — for reach

These are the big, popular tags everyone knows: #food, #travel, #cricket, #fashion. They have huge audiences, but also huge competition — your post disappears from them in minutes. Use one or two at most. They tell the platform your general topic, but they will not bring you loyal followers on their own.

2. Niche hashtags — for the right people

These are specific tags with a smaller but more interested audience: #streetfoodlahore, #budgettravelpakistan, #handmadeembroidery. Fewer people search them, but the people who do are exactly the people who will like, comment and follow. This is where most of your hashtags should come from — two or three per post.

3. Branded and community hashtags — for your own people

Make one hashtag your own, such as your channel name or a phrase your followers recognise, and use it on every post. It collects all your content in one place and lets your audience find everything you have made. Community tags — the ones a group of creators or fans share — work the same way and connect you with people making similar content.

How to find hashtags that actually fit your post

Guessing is the slowest way to find hashtags. Use these methods instead — they take minutes and they are all free:

  1. Start with your own words: Write down what your post is about in plain words — the topic, the place, the audience, the feeling. "Easy chicken biryani recipe for beginners in Pakistan" gives you #biryani, #pakistanifood, #easyrecipes and #beginnercooking before you even open a tool.
  2. Check the search bar: Type a hashtag into Instagram or TikTok search and the app suggests related tags. Look for tags that are active but not so huge that your post vanishes instantly. A tag with thousands of posts is often better for you than one with hundreds of millions.
  3. Study similar creators: Find three creators who make content like yours and look at the hashtags on their most-liked posts. Do not copy them blindly — note the patterns, then pick the ones that genuinely fit your post too.
  4. Watch what is trending — carefully: Check the trending or Discover page on TikTok and the Explore page on Instagram. A trending hashtag is only useful if your content honestly belongs to that trend. Forcing your post into an unrelated trend annoys viewers and teaches the algorithm the wrong thing about your content.

Common hashtag mistakes

  • Only using huge hashtags: #love or #instagood have hundreds of millions of posts. Your post is buried in seconds. Mix one broad tag with niche tags where you can actually be found.
  • Copying the same set every time: Platforms notice when every post carries an identical block of tags, and viewers notice too. Keep a core set per topic, but swap a few tags for each new post.
  • Using irrelevant or misleading tags: Adding #giveaway or #viral to a post that is neither is the fastest way to lose trust. People who arrive expecting one thing and find another will leave — and may report the post.
  • Hashtag stuffing: Thirty tags under a two-line caption looks desperate and is hard to read. If you need more than ten tags to describe a post, the post itself is probably unfocused.
  • Ignoring banned or broken tags: Some hashtags get restricted because of spam or misuse. If a tag shows almost no recent posts, or a warning when you tap it, drop it — it will not help your reach.
  • Forgetting your own brand tag: Without a consistent tag of your own, your posts scatter. One branded hashtag on every post builds a library your followers can browse.
  • Using hashtags that do not match the language: If your audience speaks Urdu or Hindi, a mix of English and local-language tags can help them find you. But never translate a tag word for word without checking that people actually use it — a tag nobody searches for helps nobody.

Platform-specific tips

Instagram

Put hashtags at the end of your caption so the first lines stay clean and readable. For Reels, hashtags matter even more, because Reels travel beyond your followers — pick tags that describe the video's topic, not just your account. Check which tags brought you profile visits in Instagram Insights and keep the winners.

TikTok

On TikTok, the sound and the first two seconds matter more than hashtags — but tags still help the algorithm place your video. Use the tags of the niche you want to be in, not the tags of the biggest trend of the day. If your video uses a trending sound, the sound itself already connects you to that trend; your hashtags should describe your content.

YouTube

Hashtags on YouTube are a small helper, not the main tool — your title, description and thumbnail do the heavy work. Add your three most important tags first in the description so they show above the title, and let the rest support the topic. A channel hashtag in every description (like your channel name) ties your videos together.

Frequently asked questions

How many hashtags should I use on Instagram?

Three to five relevant hashtags is a good number for most posts. Instagram allows up to 30, but a small set of tags that truly describe your post usually performs better and looks cleaner.

How many hashtags should I use on TikTok?

Three to five. TikTok captions are short, so keep the tags focused: one or two broad, one or two niche, and one trending tag only if it genuinely fits your video.

How many hashtags can I use on YouTube?

You can add up to 15 hashtags in a video description — add more than 15 and YouTube ignores all of them. The first three appear above your video title, so put your most important tags first.

Do hashtags still work in 2026?

Yes — they still help platforms understand and categorise your content, and they still help people find posts on topics they follow. What has changed is that quality matters far more than quantity: a few relevant tags beat a long list of random ones.

Should I use the same hashtags on every post?

Keep a core set per topic, but change a few tags each time. Posting an identical block of hashtags on every post for months can look automated, and it stops you learning which tags actually work for each kind of post.
