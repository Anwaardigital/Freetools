How to Split PDFs Online for Free (2026 Guide)

You need pages 12–18 of a 200-page manual. One chapter of an e-book for a friend. A single invoice out of a year's worth of statements. Sending the whole PDF is clumsy — and sometimes inappropriate. Splitting lets you pull out exactly the pages you need into a brand-new PDF. This guide walks you through splitting PDFs online for free, in your browser, with no signup.

What "splitting" a PDF means

Splitting (or extracting pages) means creating a new PDF that contains only the pages you choose from the original. Your original file is never modified — the tool copies the selected pages into a fresh document. Common uses:

  • Extract a chapter: pull one chapter out of a textbook or manual.
  • Share selectively: send one invoice from a year's statements, or your pages from a shared report.
  • Remove unwanted pages: keep pages 1–10 and drop the 40 pages of appendices you'll never read.
  • Prepare uploads: many forms and portals accept only small files — extract just the relevant pages.

What you'll need

Any modern browser — desktop or mobile. Nothing to install and no account needed. An internet connection is required the first time so the tool can load its PDF engine; the splitting itself then runs entirely on your device.

Step-by-step: split a PDF online

  1. Open the PDF Splitter. You'll see a file selection area and a "Pages to extract" text field.
  2. Select your PDF file. The tool shows the file name and size to confirm the right file loaded.
  3. Type the pages you want in the "Pages to extract" field, using this simple syntax:
    • 5 — just page 5
    • 1-3 — pages 1, 2 and 3
    • 1-3, 5, 7-9 — pages 1–3, plus page 5, plus pages 7–9
    You can mix single pages and ranges freely, separated by commas.
  4. Click "Split / Extract Pages". The tool copies your selected pages into a new document.
  5. Download the result. The new file downloads as split.pdf. Open it and confirm it contains exactly the pages you wanted, then rename it.

Understanding the page syntax

The page field is flexible, but a few rules keep things predictable:

  • Pages are numbered from 1. The first page of the PDF is page 1 (not 0).
  • Ranges use a hyphen: 4-8 means pages 4, 5, 6, 7, 8.
  • Commas separate selections: 2, 6-8, 12 extracts pages 2, 6, 7, 8 and 12.
  • Duplicates are removed automatically: 3, 3-5 won't give you page 3 twice.
  • Out-of-range pages are ignored: if your PDF has 10 pages and you type 1-15, you get pages 1–10.
  • Pages keep the order you type: 9, 1-3 produces a PDF with page 9 first, then pages 1–3 — handy for reordering.
  • Invalid input gets a helpful error: if nothing you typed matches, the tool tells you how many pages the PDF actually has so you can correct it.

Tips for clean splits

  • Know your page count first. Open the PDF in your browser or reader and note the total pages before typing ranges — it prevents off-by-ten mistakes.
  • One extraction per run. Each run produces one PDF. If you need three separate chapters, run the tool three times with different page selections.
  • Use splitting to reorder pages. Listing pages out of order (5, 1-4) gives you a reordered document — a neat trick for fixing mis-ordered scans.
  • Need the opposite? To combine several PDFs into one, use the PDF Merger. To make the result smaller, follow up with the PDF Compressor.

Privacy: your files stay on your device

Splitting happens locally in your browser — the PDF is never uploaded to a server. That's a meaningful advantage over online services that require uploads, particularly when the document contains contracts, financial records, IDs, or medical information.

Honest limitations

  • Password-protected PDFs: encrypted files typically can't be processed until unlocked.
  • One output file per run: the tool extracts one set of pages at a time; splitting a PDF into five separate files takes five quick runs.
  • Scanned pages stay scanned: splitting copies pages exactly as they are — it doesn't run OCR, so scanned image pages won't become searchable text. (To pull text out of a PDF, try the PDF to Text tool — though it needs real text, not scans.)
  • Very large PDFs are processed in your device's memory; on low-memory phones, split huge files in smaller batches.

Frequently asked questions

Is splitting PDFs online free?

Yes — completely free, unlimited, no signup, no watermarks.

Will splitting reduce quality?

No. Pages are copied into the new PDF, not re-rendered, so text and images keep their original quality.

Can I split a PDF into multiple files at once?

Not in a single click — each run creates one PDF. For multiple sections, run the tool once per section with different page ranges.

Is my document uploaded anywhere?

No. Everything happens in your browser; your files never leave your device.
