How to Convert Images Online for Free (2026 Guide)

A website that only accepts JPG. A designer asking for PNG with transparency. A modern site recommending WebP for speed. Image formats are a quiet source of friction: the picture is fine, but it's in the "wrong" wrapper. Converting an image — changing its format without changing what it looks like — takes seconds once you know how. This guide shows you how to convert images online for free, in your browser, plus which format to pick and why.

When conversion is the right move

  • Upload requirements: portals, printers, and platforms that accept only specific formats.
  • Smaller files: switching a photo from PNG to JPG or WebP can shrink it dramatically.
  • Transparency needs: logos and graphics that need a transparent background belong in PNG or WebP, not JPG.
  • Web performance: WebP images load faster than equivalent PNGs or JPGs.

Format guide: PNG vs JPG vs WebP vs BMP

The tool converts between four formats. Here's what each is for:

  • PNG — lossless quality, supports transparency. Best for logos, screenshots, graphics, and anything with text. Files are larger than JPG/WebP for photos.
  • JPEG (JPG) — lossy compression, no transparency. Best for photographs where small file size matters more than pixel-perfect quality. Every re-save loses a little quality.
  • WebP — modern format with excellent compression, supports transparency and animation. Best for web use in 2026 — supported by all major browsers. Smaller than both PNG and JPG at similar quality.
  • BMP — uncompressed, very large files, no real advantages today. Rarely needed — mostly for legacy software compatibility.

Quick rule of thumb: photos → JPG or WebP; graphics/logos/screenshots → PNG or WebP; web uploads → WebP; legacy systems → whatever they ask for.

Tips for clean conversions

  • Converting JPG → PNG won't restore quality. JPG compression discards detail permanently; wrapping it in a lossless PNG just makes a bigger file with the same quality. Convert "down" (PNG → JPG) for size, not "up" for quality.
  • Watch out for transparency. Converting a transparent PNG to JPG replaces transparency with a solid background (usually black or white) — fine for photos, bad for logos. Use PNG or WebP if transparency matters.
  • WebP is the 2026 sweet spot for anything going on the web: smaller files than JPG/PNG at comparable quality, with transparency support.
  • Your original is untouched. Conversion creates a new file; the source image stays exactly as it was.
  • Need it smaller too? After converting, shrink file size further with the Image Compressor, or change dimensions with the Image Resizer.

Honest limitations

  • Four formats only: PNG, JPEG, WebP, and BMP. Formats like TIFF, HEIC, AVIF, or SVG aren't supported — phone photos in HEIC need converting elsewhere first.
  • No quality slider: JPG/WebP output uses the browser's default quality. For fine control over compression level, use the Image Compressor.
  • One image at a time: batch conversion isn't supported.

Frequently asked questions

Is image conversion really free?

Yes — free, unlimited, no signup, no watermarks.

Which format should I choose?

Photos for sharing or upload: JPG or WebP. Graphics, logos, screenshots, or anything needing transparency: PNG or WebP. In doubt for web use: WebP.

Does converting reduce quality?

Converting to JPG is lossy (some quality traded for smaller size). PNG, WebP, and BMP conversions preserve full quality.

Are my images uploaded anywhere?

No. Conversion happens entirely in your browser; your images never leave your device.
