How to Resize Images Online for Free (2026 Guide)

A profile photo that must be exactly 400×400 pixels. A banner that's too wide for your website. A 12-megapixel phone photo you want to email without choking the recipient's inbox. Resizing — changing an image's pixel dimensions — is one of the most common image tasks there is, and you don't need Photoshop for it. This guide shows you how to resize images online for free, to exact dimensions, in your browser.

When resizing is the right move

  • Exact-size requirements: profile pictures, ID photos, marketplace listings, and ad platforms that demand specific pixel dimensions.
  • Faster websites: a 4000-pixel-wide photo displayed at 800 pixels is pure waste — resizing it down slashes load time.
  • Email and messaging: smaller dimensions mean dramatically smaller files that send in seconds.
  • Uniform galleries: making a batch of images the same size for a consistent look.

What you'll need

Any modern browser on desktop or mobile. No installation, no account, no internet needed after the page loads — resizing runs entirely on your device.

Step-by-step: resize an image online

  1. Open the Image Resizer. You'll see an image selection area.
  2. Select your image. A preview appears, and the Width and Height fields automatically fill in with the image's original dimensions — so you always know your starting point.
  3. Enter your target dimensions. Type the new width and height in pixels. Two modes:
    • Keep aspect ratio (checked, default): type just the width or just the height, and the other updates automatically to preserve the image's proportions. This is what you want 95% of the time — it prevents stretched or squished images.
    • Unchecked: width and height are independent, for when you truly need exact, non-proportional dimensions (e.g. a platform's required 1200×628 banner).
  4. Click "Resize & Download". The resized image downloads immediately, named after your original file with the new dimensions appended — e.g. photo-800x600.png.

Understanding aspect ratio

Aspect ratio is simply the relationship between width and height — 16:9, 4:3, 1:1 (square). When "Keep aspect ratio" is on, changing 4000×3000 to a width of 800 gives you 800×600 automatically, because the proportions are preserved. Turn it off only when the destination demands exact non-proportional dimensions; otherwise you'll get the dreaded stretched-face effect.

Common target sizes cheat sheet

Not sure what dimensions to type? These cover the most frequent requests: profile avatars — square, 400×400 or 800×800; ID/passport-style photos — check the issuer, commonly around 600×600 or 35×45 mm at 300 DPI (≈ 413×531 px); website hero images — 1920 pixels wide (height varies); blog/content images — 800–1200 pixels wide; email attachments — 800–1024 pixels on the longest side keeps files small; social posts — 1080×1080 square or 1080×1350 portrait for feeds. When in doubt, resize to the largest size you'll need — you can always make a smaller copy later, but enlarging a small copy loses quality.

Tips for great results

  • Downscale freely, upscale cautiously. Making images smaller is safe and sharp. Making them bigger than the original can't add detail that isn't there — the result gets soft or blurry. Upscale only modestly if you must.
  • Know your target size first. Check what the platform wants (profile: often square like 400×400; website hero: often 1920 wide; email: 800–1200 wide is plenty) before resizing.
  • Keep the original. Resizing creates a new file; your original is untouched. Keep it — you can always resize again, but you can't recover detail from a downscaled copy.
  • Need a different format too? After resizing, convert between PNG, JPEG, WebP and BMP with the Image Converter. To trim edges instead of scaling, use the Image Cropper.

Privacy: your photos stay on your device

The resize happens locally in your browser using a canvas — your image is never uploaded to any server. Family photos, IDs, screenshots with personal data: none of it leaves your device.

Honest limitations

  • Output is PNG. The resized file always downloads as a PNG (e.g. photo-800x600.png), regardless of the input format. PNG is lossless and high quality, but larger than JPEG — if you need a small JPEG, convert afterwards with the Image Converter.
  • Resizing ≠ compressing. Smaller dimensions do make smaller files, but if your goal is purely a smaller file at the same dimensions, use the Image Compressor instead.
  • No batch resizing. Images are resized one at a time — fine for a handful, tedious for hundreds.

Frequently asked questions

Is resizing images online free?

Yes — free, unlimited, no signup, no watermarks.

Will resizing reduce image quality?

Downscaling keeps images looking sharp. Upscaling beyond the original size softens detail — keep enlargements modest.

What format does the resized image download as?

PNG, with the new dimensions in the file name (e.g. photo-800x600.png).

Are my images uploaded anywhere?

No. Resizing happens entirely in your browser; your images never leave your device.
