Image Cropper

Crop images to any rectangular region you choose with a simple selection. Useful for removing unwanted edges or focusing on the subject. Free, unlimited, private — works entirely in your browser.

How to use

  1. Upload your image by clicking the upload area or dragging it into the tool.
  2. Drag the corners or edges of the selection box to choose the area you want to keep — or pick a preset aspect ratio like 1:1, 4:3 or 16:9.
  3. Fine-tune the selection by dragging the box itself to reposition it over your image.
  4. Click Crop, preview the result, then download your cropped image.

Will cropping reduce my image quality?

Cropping itself is lossless — it simply discards pixels outside your selection. The remaining pixels are untouched. Quality only drops if you then upscale the cropped region beyond its original size, so crop from the highest-resolution original you have.

What are aspect ratios and which should I use?

An aspect ratio is the proportional relationship between width and height. Use 1:1 for Instagram posts and profile pictures, 16:9 for YouTube thumbnails and widescreen banners, 4:3 for standard photos, and 9:16 for stories and reels. Free-form cropping is best when no platform dictates the shape.

Can I crop to an exact pixel size?

The crop box defines proportions; the output keeps the original resolution of the selected area. If you need exact dimensions (e.g. exactly 1200x630 for Open Graph images), crop first for composition, then use the Image Resizer to hit precise pixel dimensions.

Does the cropper work on transparent PNGs?

Yes — transparency is preserved when you download as PNG. If you download as JPG, transparent areas will be filled (usually white), since JPG doesn't support transparency.

Great photos are often hiding inside mediocre ones — cropping out distractions, straightening the composition and focusing on the subject can transform an image in seconds. How it works

Upload an image and an interactive selection box appears over it. Drag the handles to resize the box, drag inside it to move it, and the tool shows you a live preview of what the final image will contain. When you confirm, only the pixels inside the box are kept — everything else is discarded. The operation runs on the full-resolution original, so the cropped result is as sharp as the source allows.

Composition tips

  • Rule of thirds: imagine the frame divided into a 3x3 grid and place key subjects along the lines or intersections rather than dead center.
  • Cut the clutter: photobombers, messy backgrounds and empty sky rarely add anything — crop them out ruthlessly.
  • Leave breathing room: when cropping portraits, don't slice through joints (elbows, knees); leave a little space around the subject.
  • Mind the platform: crop to the aspect ratio of where the image will live — a perfect 1:1 Instagram crop looks awkward stretched into a 16:9 banner.

Common use cases

Profile pictures: crop headshots to 1:1, centering the face slightly above middle. Thumbnails: 16:9 crops with the subject large and high-contrast read best at tiny sizes. Product photos: tight crops that fill the frame with the product convert better than wide shots with empty background. Document scans: crop away desk edges and shadows for clean, professional-looking scans.

Workflow advice

Crop before you compress or resize — there's no point optimizing pixels you're about to throw away. Work from the original file, not a previously cropped copy, to avoid compounding quality loss. And if you're unsure about a tight crop, save a looser version too; you can always crop more later, but you can't uncrop.
