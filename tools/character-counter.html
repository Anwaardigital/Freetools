FAQ

Is it really free?

Yes — every tool on this site is 100% free with no limits, no signup and no watermarks.

Is my data private?

Absolutely. All processing happens locally in your browser. Your files are never uploaded to any server.

Do spaces count as characters?

Both figures are shown: characters with spaces and characters without spaces. Social platforms and SMS count spaces, while some essay requirements exclude them — so pick the number that matches your limit.

What are the character limits for popular platforms?

As of 2026: X/Twitter posts 280 characters, Instagram captions 2,200, Facebook posts 63,206, LinkedIn posts 3,000, YouTube titles 100, meta descriptions ~155–160, SMS segments 160 (GSM) or 70 (Unicode). The counter helps you stay inside all of these.

How is reading time calculated?

Reading time assumes an average adult speed of about 200–250 words per minute for silent reading; speaking time uses roughly 130–150 words per minute, the typical pace for presentations and voiceovers. Your audience may vary, but it is a solid planning estimate.

Does it count emojis and special characters correctly?

Yes. The counter is Unicode-aware, so emojis, accented characters, and non-Latin scripts (Urdu, Arabic, Chinese) are counted as single characters each — unlike naive counters that miscount multi-byte characters.

Complete Guide to Counting Characters and Words

Character and word counts quietly govern huge parts of modern writing: a tweet that is three characters too long, a college essay that misses the word minimum, a meta description that Google truncates mid-sentence. This guide explains the counts that matter, the limits on major platforms, and how to write tightly within them.

Characters vs words vs everything else

Characters are the finest-grained limit — every letter, space, and punctuation mark. Words matter for essays, articles, and assignments. Sentences and paragraphs reveal structure: a 500-word wall of two paragraphs reads very differently from ten short ones. Reading time translates abstract counts into audience experience — a "5-minute read" sets expectations instantly. Professionals check all of these, not just one number.

Limits worth memorising

Social: X/Twitter 280 characters, Threads 500, Instagram captions 2,200 (but only ~125 show before "more"), LinkedIn 3,000. Messaging: a single SMS segment holds 160 GSM characters but only 70 if you use Unicode (emojis, Urdu/Arabic script) — one emoji can triple your SMS cost by forcing multi-segment messages. Web: title tags display ~60 characters, meta descriptions ~155–160, YouTube titles 100. Academic: abstracts commonly 150–300 words; check your institution's rules on whether references count.

Writing within limits: practical tactics

Cut filler phrases ("in order to" → "to", "due to the fact that" → "because"). Prefer strong verbs over adverb-verb pairs ("ran quickly" → "sprinted"). For headlines, front-load the key words — truncation cuts from the right. For SMS and tweets, replace "and" with "&" only where the tone allows. Read the text aloud: if you stumble, the sentence is too long regardless of the count.

Keyword density and SEO

The density panel shows how often each word appears relative to total words. There is no magic percentage, but if your target keyword appears in every other sentence, the text will read as stuffed to humans and search engines alike. Aim for natural variation with synonyms — and remember that modern SEO rewards covering a topic thoroughly over repeating one phrase.
