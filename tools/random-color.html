FAQ

Is it really free?

Yes — every tool on this site is 100% free with no limits, no signup and no watermarks.

Is my data private?

Absolutely. All processing happens locally in your browser. Your files are never uploaded to any server.

How is the randomness generated?

Colors are picked using your browser's cryptographically secure random number generator (crypto.getRandomValues), so every one of the 16.7 million possible colors has an equal chance — no patterns, no repeats bias.

What do HEX, RGB, and HSL mean?

HEX (#FF5733) is the web standard for CSS. RGB (255, 87, 51) gives red/green/blue channel values. HSL (11°, 100%, 60%) describes hue, saturation, and lightness — designers often prefer HSL for tweaking shades.

Can I get only certain kinds of colors?

Use the filters: pastels for soft UI backgrounds, vivid for bold accents, dark for moody themes. Filtering narrows the random range instead of picking from all 16.7 million colors.

How do I check if two colors work together?

Generate a few candidates, then check their contrast ratio for text readability (4.5:1 minimum for body text per WCAG). Complementary colors sit opposite on the color wheel; analogous neighbors feel calmer.

Random Color Generator — Complete Guide

Staring at a blank canvas not knowing which color to pick is a universal designer problem. A Random Color Generator breaks the block: one click gives you a genuinely random color with copy-ready codes, and constraints like "pastels only" keep the chaos useful.

Understanding color codes

  • HEX (#3A86FF): six hexadecimal digits — the lingua franca of web CSS. Copy-paste straight into stylesheets, Figma, or Canva.
  • RGB (58, 134, 255): how much red, green, and blue light to mix. The foundation of every screen color.
  • HSL (217°, 100%, 62%): hue (position on the wheel), saturation (intensity), lightness. Easiest model for humans to adjust — want it darker? Lower the L.

Practical use cases

  • Web design: discover accent colors for buttons, links, and highlights that you wouldn't have picked deliberately.
  • Branding exploration: generate dozens of candidates fast, shortlist the interesting ones, then refine.
  • Data visualization: build distinct palettes for charts where every series needs its own recognizable color.
  • Creative coding & games: randomize particle effects, avatars, or generative art with uniform randomness.
  • CSS gradients: grab two random colors and drop them into a linear-gradient for instant backgrounds.

Working with random colors like a pro

  • Constrain the range: unlimited randomness often yields muddy browns. Pastel, vivid, or dark filters produce usable results far more often.
  • Check contrast: beautiful means nothing if text is unreadable. Aim for WCAG AA contrast (4.5:1) for body text.
  • Build palettes: lock one color you love, then generate companions. Tools like coolors-style lock-and-roll workflows were invented for exactly this.
  • Note the codes: copy HEX values into your design system immediately — "that nice blue from earlier" is otherwise lost forever.

Color psychology quick reference

Blues signal trust and calm (banks, healthcare); reds create urgency and appetite (sales, food); greens suggest growth and eco-friendliness; purples feel creative and premium; yellows grab attention but tire the eyes in large areas. Use random generation for discovery, then apply psychology for the final pick.

Accessibility reminder

About 1 in 12 men has some color-vision deficiency. Never rely on color alone to convey meaning — pair it with labels, icons, or patterns, and test palettes with a color-blindness simulator before shipping.
