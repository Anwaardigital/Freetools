Link Shortener

Turn long, ugly URLs into short, shareable links in one click via a free shortening service (da.gd / is.gd). Useful for social posts and SMS where space is tight. Free, unlimited, no signup — nothing is stored on this site.

How to use

  1. Paste your long URL into the "Long URL" field above — no account needed. If you forget the https:// part, it is added automatically.
  2. Click Shorten Link. Your browser contacts the free da.gd service over a secure HTTPS connection (with is.gd, v.gd and TinyURL tried automatically as fallbacks).
  3. Your short link appears instantly as a clickable link. Press Copy Link to copy it to your clipboard.
  4. Share it anywhere — social posts, SMS, bios or messages — wherever space is tight and long URLs break.

FAQ

Is it really free?

Yes — every tool on this site is 100% free with no limits and no signup.

How does it work?

Your URL is sent to a free shortening service (da.gd / is.gd) over a secure connection, which returns a compact link. Nothing is stored on this site.

Do I need an account or API key?

No. Unlike many commercial shorteners, this tool needs no signup, no login and no API key. Just paste and shorten — as many links as you like.

Are my shortened links stored anywhere?

No. Nothing is stored on this website. Your long URL is sent over HTTPS to the shortening service, which returns a short link instantly. The short-to-long mapping lives with the shortening service (da.gd / is.gd), not with us.

Do short links expire?

Links created through da.gd and is.gd have no set expiry date and generally keep working indefinitely. However, they depend on the shortening service staying online — for links you need forever (like on a printed flyer), keep the original full URL safe as well.

Is it safe to click a short link someone sends me?

Short links hide the destination, which is exactly the trick phishing attacks use. Before clicking an unexpected short link, check who sent it and expand it first with a URL expander or link-preview service so you can see the real destination.

Why Shorten Links — and How to Do It Safely

Long URLs are ugly, hard to share and easy to break. A link copied from a search result or a shopping site can run to hundreds of characters; paste it into an SMS or a social media bio and it gets cut off, wrapped across lines, or simply rejected. A link shortener solves this by creating a tiny alias — a short, clean URL that redirects anyone who clicks it to the original long address. This tool does that in one click, with no account and no cost.

Here is what happens technically: when you click "Shorten Link", your browser sends the long URL over an encrypted HTTPS connection to the free da.gd shortening service. That service stores the mapping and returns a short link like da.gd/Ab12. If da.gd is unreachable, the tool automatically tries is.gd, v.gd and TinyURL — so you almost never get stuck. Nothing is stored on this website; the mapping lives with the shortening service, and your link works from any device, anywhere.

When a short link is the right choice

Short links shine wherever space or looks matter: text messages with character limits, social media posts and bios, QR-code-free verbal sharing ("go to da.gd slash Ab12"), printed materials like flyers and business cards, and chat messages where a 300-character URL looks unprofessional. They also make analytics-friendly sharing easier — many people re-share short links without mangling them. For maximum reliability, test every short link once in a private window before publishing it.

Safety first: short links and phishing

The same feature that makes short links convenient — hiding the destination — is what phishers exploit. Never click a short link from an unknown sender without checking it first: hover to see nothing (the real URL is hidden), so use a URL expander or link-preview service to reveal the destination before you visit. Legitimate businesses rarely send login links through generic shorteners. And one rule of thumb: don't use short links for documents people must trust long-term (contracts, certificates) — if the shortening service ever goes down, the link dies with it, so keep the original full URL filed safely.
